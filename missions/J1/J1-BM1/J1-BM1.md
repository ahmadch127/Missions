## Learning Objective

Apply budgeting, needs-versus-wants, saving, and emergency-planning skills to manage a limited amount of money for one complete month.

## Primary Combined Misconception

A successful monthly budget only needs to cover planned expenses and does not need savings or preparation for emergencies.

## Secondary Misconceptions

* Wants must always be removed completely from a good budget.
* Borrowing is always the best solution when an unexpected expense occurs.
* Saving should only happen when money remains at the end of the month.

## Key Takeaway

**A successful month balances needs, wants, savings, and unexpected expenses.**

# 2. Boss Mission Overview

The learner manages Rs. 50,000 for one month, builds a realistic budget, sorts needs and wants, decides how to use extra income, and handles an essential phone repair. Earlier choices change the resources available later.

This Boss Mission appears after Emergency Fund because it combines the journey’s main skills in one connected challenge. The final result measures needs coverage, budget quality, saving behaviour, emergency readiness, debt pressure, and adaptability.

The experience should feel challenging but manageable. Mistakes create useful consequences and replay opportunities rather than punishment.

# Stage 1 — Build the Budget

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J1-BM1-ST1
**Timeline:** Day 1
**Interaction:** Budget Builder

**Concepts Assessed:**

* Budgeting
* Essential-expense prioritisation
* Saving first

## Situation

Your Rs. 50,000 must last the month. Food, transport, mobile data, and work expenses have minimum costs. You also decide how much to save, spend on enjoyment, or leave as a flexible buffer.

## Learner Objective

Create a budget that:

* Covers every essential minimum
* Does not exceed Rs. 50,000
* Includes saving or financial flexibility
* May include affordable enjoyment

## Budget Categories

### Required Categories

| Category      |    Minimum |    Maximum |
| ------------- | ---------: | ---------: |
| Food          | Rs. 15,000 | Rs. 22,000 |
| Transport     |  Rs. 8,000 | Rs. 14,000 |
| Mobile data   |  Rs. 2,000 |  Rs. 5,000 |
| Work expenses |  Rs. 7,000 | Rs. 12,000 |

### Optional Categories

| Category                | Minimum |    Maximum |
| ----------------------- | ------: | ---------: |
| Savings                 |   Rs. 0 | Rs. 15,000 |
| Entertainment and wants |   Rs. 0 | Rs. 12,000 |

## Remaining Balance

**Monthly Buffer = Rs. 50,000 − all allocations**

The budget may leave money unallocated as a flexible buffer.

## Example Balanced Budget

| Category       |         Amount |
| -------------- | -------------: |
| Food           |     Rs. 15,000 |
| Transport      |      Rs. 8,000 |
| Mobile data    |      Rs. 2,000 |
| Work expenses  |      Rs. 7,000 |
| Savings        |     Rs. 10,000 |
| Entertainment  |      Rs. 5,000 |
| Monthly buffer |      Rs. 3,000 |
| **Total**      | **Rs. 50,000** |

## Valid Completion States

### Balanced Budget

**Classification:** Recommended

**Immediate Effect:**

* Essential needs are covered.
* Savings increase by the selected savings amount.
* Entertainment money becomes available.
* Unallocated money becomes the monthly buffer.

**Later Effect:**
Savings, entertainment money, and the buffer may help pay for the phone repair.

### Valid Budget With No Entertainment

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Effect:**
The plan may create stronger savings but provide no room for enjoyment.

**Trade-Off:**
The budget may feel restrictive even though the maths works.

### Essential Category Below Minimum

**Classification:** Invalid

**Feedback:**
One essential expense is not covered.

**Retry:**
Keep valid allocations and highlight the category below its minimum.

### Total Above Rs. 50,000

**Classification:** Invalid

**Feedback:**
The budget uses more money than is available.

**Retry:**
Keep valid allocations and show the exact overspending amount.

## Recommended Decision Logic

The generally strongest budget:

* Covers all essential minimums
* Saves something
* Leaves affordable room for enjoyment or a buffer
* Does not assume every rupee must be spent

There is no single perfect allocation.

# Stage 2 — Need, Want, or Depends?

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J1-BM1-ST2
**Timeline:** Week 1
**Interaction:** Sort Categories

**Concepts Assessed:**

* Needs versus wants
* Essential-expense prioritisation
* Context-dependent spending

## Situation

Your budget is set, but real purchases do not always arrive with clear labels. Sort six expenses before deciding what should be protected when money becomes tight.

## Learner Objective

Sort every expense into:

* Need
* Want
* Depends on the Situation

## Categories

### Need

Usually essential for health, safety, work, or basic daily life.

### Want

Optional or replaceable without harming basic needs.

### Depends on the Situation

Its role changes with purpose, circumstances, and available alternatives.

## Sortable Items

| Item                                          | Correct Category | Explanation                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Prescribed medicine                           | Need             | Supports health and should normally be protected                    |
| New gaming headphones                         | Want             | Usually optional when working audio is already available            |
| Bus fare to work                              | Need             | Necessary for reaching work                                         |
| Restaurant meal                               | Depends          | Could be optional or necessary when no practical alternative exists |
| Internet package                              | Depends          | May be essential for work but optional as a premium upgrade         |
| Premium shoes when usable shoes are available | Want             | The premium upgrade is optional                                     |

## Ambiguity Rule

The learner should judge an expense by:

* Its purpose
* Available alternatives
* Consequences of not paying
* Current circumstances

## Scoring

* 6 correct: 20 points
* 5 correct: 16 points
* 4 correct: 12 points
* 3 correct: 8 points
* Fewer than 3: 4 points after completion

## Updated State Screen

**Screen Title:** Priorities Identified

**Learning Message:**
Needs should be protected, wants can be managed, and some expenses depend on context.

**Next Teaser:**
An extra Rs. 5,000 is about to arrive.

## Retry Behaviour

Keep correct items in place. Return only incorrect items with one short clue.

## Accessibility Alternative

Present one item at a time with three labelled category buttons.

# Stage 3 — Saving First

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J1-BM1-ST3
**Timeline:** Week 2
**Interaction:** Decision Tree

**Concepts Assessed:**

* Saving first
* Emergency preparation
* Balancing saving and enjoyment

## Situation

You earn an unexpected Rs. 5,000 from a small freelance task. Your savings, emergency fund, buffer, and entertainment budget carry forward from earlier choices.

## Learner Objective

Decide how the extra income should support the rest of the month.

## Choice 1 — Save All Rs. 5,000 for Emergencies

**Classification:** Recommended when emergency readiness is low

**Immediate Effect:**

* Emergency fund: +Rs. 5,000
* Wants purchased: no change

**Short-Term Consequence:**
Emergency readiness improves.

**Later Consequence:**
The phone repair may require less general savings or debt.

**Score:** 20 points

## Choice 2 — Spend All Rs. 5,000 on a Want

**Classification:** Risky but valid

**Immediate Effect:**

* Optional spending: +Rs. 5,000
* Wants purchased: +1
* Emergency fund: unchanged

**Later Consequence:**
The phone repair may place greater pressure on savings or borrowing.

**Score:** 6 points

## Choice 3 — Save Rs. 3,000 and Spend Rs. 2,000

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Effect:**

* Emergency fund: +Rs. 3,000
* Optional spending: +Rs. 2,000
* Wants purchased: +1

**Later Consequence:**
The repair will still require another funding source.

**Score:** 18 points

## Choice 4 — Keep Rs. 5,000 Available as a Buffer

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Effect:**

* Monthly buffer: +Rs. 5,000

**Later Consequence:**
The money can help with the repair, but it remains easier to spend before then.

**Score:** 16 points

## Decision Logic

Saving all is generally strongest because the learner begins with no emergency fund.

Other choices may be reasonable when:

* Existing savings are already strong
* A necessary expense is expected soon
* The learner accepts slower emergency-fund progress in exchange for enjoyment

# Stage 4 — The Phone Emergency

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J1-BM1-ST4
**Timeline:** Week 3
**Interaction:** Predict Outcome and Decision Tree

**Concepts Assessed:**

* Emergency-fund use
* Budget adjustment
* Needs versus wants
* Responsible borrowing

## Final Situation

Your phone stops working. The Rs. 8,000 repair is essential for work and communication.

Your available emergency fund, savings, buffer, and optional money depend on decisions made earlier.

## Prediction Question

Which approach is most likely to protect essential needs and limit new debt?

### Option 1

Use prepared emergency money and accessible savings.

**Educationally Preferred:** Yes

### Option 2

Borrow immediately without checking available resources.

### Option 3

Skip another essential payment.

### Option 4

Ignore the repair even though the phone is needed for work.

## Prediction Explanation

Prepared savings and flexible budget categories can reduce pressure, but the strongest choice still depends on the balances available.

## Final Decision Choices

### Choice 1 — Use Emergency Money First, Then Accessible Savings

**Classification:** Recommended when enough money is available

**Funding Order:**

1. Emergency fund
2. General savings
3. Monthly buffer

**Immediate Effect:**
The repair is paid from money prepared or saved earlier.

**Long-Term Consequence:**
Savings fall and may need rebuilding.

**Validation:**
The combined sources must total at least Rs. 8,000.

### Choice 2 — Borrow Rs. 8,000

**Classification:** Risky but sometimes reasonable

**Immediate Effect:**

* Debt: +Rs. 8,000
* Repair completed
* Savings remain unchanged

**Long-Term Consequence:**
Future income must cover repayment.

**When Reasonable:**
Accessible resources are insufficient and the repair is essential.

### Choice 3 — Skip Transport Payments

**Classification:** Risky

**Immediate Effect:**

* Missed essential payment: Rs. 8,000
* Essential-needs status: not fully covered

**Long-Term Consequence:**
Reaching work may become difficult.

**Educational Feedback:**
This shifts the emergency into another essential problem.

### Choice 4 — Reduce Optional Spending and Use Savings for the Rest

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Funding Order:**

1. Optional spending available
2. Monthly buffer
3. General savings

**Immediate Effect:**
The phone is repaired without debt when enough flexible resources exist.

**Long-Term Consequence:**
The month includes less enjoyment and lower savings.

## Recommended Reasoning

* Use emergency savings for an essential unexpected expense when available.
* Reducing optional spending is a strong alternative when the emergency fund is insufficient.
* Borrowing may be necessary when no accessible resources remain.
* Skipping another essential payment usually creates a second problem.

# 15. Performance Scoring System

**Maximum:** 25 points

* 25: Essentials covered, saving included, budget affordable, and enjoyment or a buffer remains

* 15: Essentials covered, but no money saved

**Maximum:** 20 points

* 6 correct: 20
* 5 correct: 16
* 4 correct: 12
* 3 correct: 8
* Fewer than 3: 4

## Dimension 3 — Saving Behaviour

**Maximum:** 20 points

* Save all extra income: 20
* Save part and spend part: 18
* Keep it as a buffer: 16
* Spend all: 6

Up to four points may be deducted when Stage 1 contains no saving allocation.

## Dimension 4 — Emergency Response

**Maximum:** 25 points

* Use emergency money and accessible savings: 25
* Reduce optional spending and use savings: 22
* Borrow when necessary: 10
* Skip another essential payment: 0

## Dimension 5 — Financial Adaptability

**Maximum:** 10 points

* Final response fits earlier resources and no need is missed: 10
* Avoidable debt is used despite sufficient resources: 6
* Debt is created but essential needs remain covered: 3
* An essential obligation is missed: 0

# 19. Boss Mission Quiz

## Question 1 — Combined Recognition

**Question:**
Which monthly plan uses budgeting and emergency preparation together?

### Options

1. Cover planned bills and spend every remaining rupee
2. **Cover needs, save early, and include affordable wants**
3. Reduce food spending to increase savings
4. Plan to borrow whenever anything unexpected happens

**Correct Answer:**
Cover needs, save early, and include affordable wants

**Correct Explanation:**
A balanced plan covers essential needs, saves before optional spending, and still allows affordable enjoyment.

**Incorrect Feedback:**

* **Option 1:** The plan covers known costs but leaves no protection for surprises.
* **Option 3:** Saving should not make essential needs unaffordable.
* **Option 4:** Borrowing may help, but it creates a future obligation.

**Concepts Tested:**
Budgeting, saving first, emergency preparation

**Misconception Corrected:**
A budget only needs to cover planned expenses.

## Question 2 — Combined Misconception

**Question:**
Why should a monthly budget include savings before the month ends?

### Options

1. Because every month guarantees an emergency
2. **Because saving early protects money from being fully spent**
3. Because wants are always irresponsible
4. Because savings remove the need for a budget

**Correct Answer:**
Because saving early protects money from being fully spent

**Correct Explanation:**
Saving first turns saving into a planned action instead of depending on money being left over.

**Incorrect Feedback:**

* **Option 1:** An emergency is not guaranteed, but preparation creates options.
* **Option 3:** Affordable wants can fit after needs and future priorities are protected.
* **Option 4:** Savings work best as part of a complete budget.

**Concepts Tested:**
Saving first and budgeting

**Misconception Corrected:**
Saving should only happen when money remains at the end.

## Question 3 — New Application

**Question:**
A Rs. 6,000 medical cost appears. You have Rs. 3,000 emergency savings, Rs. 4,000 optional money, and no debt. What is the strongest response?

### Options

1. Skip transport and keep the optional money
2. **Use emergency savings and reduce optional spending**
3. Borrow the full amount immediately
4. Ignore the cost because it was not budgeted

**Correct Answer:**
Use emergency savings and reduce optional spending

**Correct Explanation:**
This combines emergency preparation and flexible spending without creating debt or missing another essential need.

**Incorrect Feedback:**

* **Option 1:** This protects a want by leaving another essential need uncovered.
* **Option 3:** Borrowing may be needed when resources are insufficient, but accessible money can cover this cost.
* **Option 4:** An essential unexpected cost still requires a workable response.

**Concepts Tested:**
Emergency savings, needs versus wants, budget adjustment

**Misconception Corrected:**
Borrowing is always the best response to an unexpected expense.
