# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J1-M7
**Journey:** Become Money Smart
**Mission Title:** Emergency Fund
**Core Concept:** An emergency fund is money saved specifically for unexpected and necessary expenses.
**Target Age Group:** Kids 8–12
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand why setting aside money for unexpected expenses helps protect regular spending plans and important financial priorities.

**Primary Misconception:**
Emergencies are too rare or unpredictable to prepare for.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Borrowing money should always be the first response to an emergency.
* Emergency savings can be used for planned or optional purchases.

**Key Takeaway:**
Emergency funds protect normal plans from unexpected costs.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Decision Tree

**Interaction Title:** Protect the Monthly Plan

**Instruction:**
Explore all four choices. Follow each consequence and decide which option best handles this emergency.

## Starting Node

Your phone breaks seven days before an important exam.

The repair costs **Rs. 8,000**, and the phone is needed for studying and family communication.

## Educational Assumptions

* The repair is necessary and cannot safely wait until after the exam.
* The regular monthly money is already assigned to food, transport, school costs, and saving.
* A trusted adult is involved in the repair, borrowing, or selling decision.
* The emergency saving jar contains at least Rs. 8,000.
* The outcomes are simplified for learning and may differ in real situations.

---

## Choice 1 — Use Emergency Savings

**Choice:**
Pay the repair cost from the emergency saving jar.

**Consequence:**
The phone is repaired. Food, transport, school costs, and normal savings remain unchanged. The emergency jar becomes Rs. 8,000 lower and will need to be rebuilt.

**Follow-Up Node:**
What did the emergency fund protect?

### Follow-Up Options

1. **The regular monthly plan**
2. A planned entertainment purchase

**Recommended Follow-Up Answer:**
The regular monthly plan

**Terminal Outcome:**
**Emergency handled with prepared money:** The necessary repair is paid without skipping essentials or creating repayment.

---

## Choice 2 — Borrow Immediately

**Choice:**
Ask a trusted family member to lend Rs. 8,000.

**Consequence:**
The phone can be repaired, but the borrowed money must be repaid from future money. This may reduce what is available for future goals or enjoyable spending.

**Follow-Up Node:**
What new responsibility appears?

### Follow-Up Options

1. **Repaying the borrowed money**
2. Receiving an extra Rs. 8,000 permanently

**Recommended Follow-Up Answer:**
Repaying the borrowed money

**Terminal Outcome:**
**Emergency handled with future pressure:** Borrowing may help when no emergency fund is available, but it moves part of the cost into the future.

---

## Choice 3 — Skip Important Expenses

**Choice:**
Use money planned for food, transport, or school supplies.

**Consequence:**
The phone is repaired, but an essential part of the monthly plan is no longer covered. A second problem may appear before the month ends.

**Follow-Up Node:**
What is the hidden cost?

### Follow-Up Options

1. **An important need becomes underfunded**
2. The repair becomes free

**Recommended Follow-Up Answer:**
An important need becomes underfunded

**Terminal Outcome:**
**One emergency creates another problem:** The phone is repaired, but essential spending is disrupted.

---

## Choice 4 — Sell Something

**Choice:**
With an adult’s help, try to sell an item you own.

**Consequence:**
A sale may provide some or all of the repair money. However, finding a buyer may take time, and you may lose an item you still use or value.

**Follow-Up Node:**
What is uncertain?

### Follow-Up Options

1. **Whether the item sells in time for enough money**
2. Whether the phone needs repair

**Recommended Follow-Up Answer:**
Whether the item sells in time for enough money

**Terminal Outcome:**
**Emergency may be handled with a trade-off:** Selling can help in some situations, but the result is not guaranteed and the item is given up.

---

## Correct or Recommended Completion State

The learner explores all four branches and identifies:

* **Use emergency savings:** Recommended in this scenario
* **Borrow immediately:** Possible, but creates repayment
* **Skip important expenses:** Repairs the phone but disrupts essentials
* **Sell something:** May help, but takes time and gives up an item

## Recommended Path

**Use emergency savings**

This option handles the necessary repair while protecting food, transport, school costs, and the normal saving plan.

## Trade-Off Summary

* **Emergency savings:** Protects the regular plan but reduces the emergency fund until it is rebuilt.
* **Borrowing:** Provides money now but creates repayment later.
* **Skipping essentials:** Pays the repair cost but leaves another important need uncovered.
* **Selling something:** May provide money but can take time and requires giving up an item.
* The best response can depend on the situation. In this activity, emergency savings are available and are designed for this type of cost.

## Validation Method

* Check each follow-up response after submission.
* Keep correctly completed branches marked.
* Complete the interaction after all four paths are explored.
* Accept **Use emergency savings** as the recommended final choice.

## Success Feedback

Strong decision. The emergency fund handles the surprise while protecting the money already planned for important needs.

## Incorrect Feedback

Look at what happens after the repair. Does the choice protect the regular plan, create repayment, or leave another need uncovered?

## Incomplete Feedback

Explore all four paths before choosing the best response.

## Retry Behaviour

Keep completed branches visible. Return the learner only to the incorrect follow-up node, show one short hint, and allow unlimited retries.

## Accessibility Alternative

Present the decision tree as four numbered, linear paths. Each path includes the choice, consequence, follow-up question, and return button.

**Screen-reader instruction:**
Choose a path, listen to its consequence, answer the follow-up question, and return to explore the other paths.

**Primary Button Label:** Finish the Decision

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** A Cushion for Real Surprises

**Explanation:**
You explored four ways to handle an unexpected repair. An **emergency fund** is money saved specifically for necessary costs you did not plan. Using it can protect food, transport, school costs, and saving goals from disruption. Borrowing or selling something may sometimes help, but both have trade-offs. Skipping essentials can create another problem. Emergency money is not for planned treats or upgrades. It is a safety cushion for genuine surprises, built a little at a time.

**Explain It Simply:**
Emergency money protects your normal plan when necessary surprises happen.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which situation is the best use of emergency savings?

### Options

1. **Repairing glasses that suddenly break**
2. Buying a planned birthday gift
3. Upgrading to a newer game
4. Purchasing extra snacks

**Correct Answer:**
Repairing glasses that suddenly break

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The broken glasses are unexpected and necessary. Repairing them helps the person see properly and continue normal daily activities.

**Incorrect Feedback — Buying a planned birthday gift:**
A planned gift should usually have its own spending plan rather than using emergency money.

**Incorrect Feedback — Upgrading to a newer game:**
A game upgrade is optional and can be planned separately.

**Incorrect Feedback — Purchasing extra snacks:**
Extra snacks are optional and are not an unexpected necessary expense.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Emergency savings can be used for planned or optional purchases.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Nothing unexpected has happened for several months. Does that mean emergency savings are useless?

### Options

1. **No, the money is ready if a necessary surprise happens**
2. Yes, because emergencies happen every month
3. Yes, so it should all be spent on wants
4. No, because it guarantees nothing will break

**Correct Answer:**
No, the money is ready if a necessary surprise happens

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Emergencies do not follow a schedule. The fund is useful because it is available before an unexpected necessary cost appears.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because emergencies happen every month:**
Emergencies may be rare and unpredictable. They do not need to happen every month for preparation to be useful.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, so it should all be spent on wants:**
Using the full fund for wants removes the protection it was created to provide.

**Incorrect Feedback — No, because it guarantees nothing will break:**
An emergency fund cannot prevent problems. It helps handle their financial effect.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Emergencies are too rare or unpredictable to prepare for.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
Ayesha has Rs. 3,000 in emergency savings. Her necessary school shoes suddenly tear and cost Rs. 2,000 to replace. A toy is also on sale. What is the strongest plan?

### Options

1. **Use Rs. 2,000 for the shoes and keep Rs. 1,000 for future emergencies**
2. Buy the toy and ignore the shoes
3. Borrow first without checking the emergency fund
4. Spend all Rs. 3,000 because an emergency happened

**Correct Answer:**
Use Rs. 2,000 for the shoes and keep Rs. 1,000 for future emergencies

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The shoes are an unexpected and necessary expense. Only the required amount needs to be used, leaving some protection for another surprise.

**Incorrect Feedback — Buy the toy and ignore the shoes:**
The toy is optional, while safe school shoes are necessary in this situation.

**Incorrect Feedback — Borrow first without checking the emergency fund:**
Borrowing creates repayment. Ayesha already has money saved for this type of necessary surprise.

**Incorrect Feedback — Spend all Rs. 3,000 because an emergency happened:**
An emergency fund should cover the necessary cost. The unused amount can remain ready for another surprise.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Borrowing money should always be the first response to an emergency.
