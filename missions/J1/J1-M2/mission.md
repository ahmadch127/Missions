Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M2
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: Needs vs Wants
Core Concept: Needs are essential for health, safety, education, and daily living, while wants are optional purchases that make life more enjoyable.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Distinguish essential expenses from optional purchases and understand why needs usually come first.
Primary Misconception:
Every item a person strongly desires is a need.
Secondary Misconceptions:
Wants are always bad or wasteful.
An expensive item must be more important than a cheaper one.
Key Takeaway:
Needs usually come first, but wants are not automatically bad.

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Sort Categories
Interaction Title: Sort the Spending List
Instruction:
Place each item under Need or Want for this situation.
Scenario Assumptions
For this activity:
The medicine is required for the person’s health.
School transport is the available way to attend school.
The child already has safe, usable everyday shoes and food at home.
These details matter because an item can be a need in one situation and a want in another.

Placement Rules
Each item must be placed in exactly one category.
The learner should use the stated scenario details.
There is one intended arrangement for this simplified story.
Context-Dependent Items
Shoes
Basic shoes may be a need if a child has no safe pair. A premium upgrade can be a want.
Food
Nutritious food is a need. Extra treats or snacks may be wants when regular food is available.
Transport
Transport may be a need when it is necessary for school, safety, or essential daily activities.
Explanation of Ambiguity
Needs and wants can change with the situation. This activity uses the details provided, not a rule that applies to every person.
Validation Method
Check every item after submission. Mark each placement as either correct or needing another look.
Success Feedback
Well sorted. You protected health and education first while recognising that fun choices can still matter later.
Incorrect Feedback
A few items need another look. Ask whether each one is essential in this situation or can safely wait.
Incomplete Feedback
Sort all five items before checking your answer.
Retry Behaviour
Keep correctly sorted items in place. Return misplaced items to the centre, show their short explanations, and allow unlimited retries.
Accessibility Alternative
Let the learner select an item and choose Need or Want using buttons or a dropdown.
Screen-reader instruction:

6. Concept Reveal
Reveal Title: Important First, Enjoyable Later
Explanation:
You sorted medicine and school transport as needs because, in this story, they support health and education. Premium shoes, snacks, and a game subscription are wants because they can be delayed without stopping essential daily activities. A need is something essential for health, safety, education, or daily living. A want is optional and adds enjoyment. Wants are not bad. The useful habit is to cover important needs first, then choose wants with the money left.
Suggested Visual:
Medicine and school transport sit safely in a First basket. The three want items wait beside a saving jar.
Explain It Simply:
Cover important needs first, then choose wants with what remains.

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Sana’s school shoes no longer fit. She also wants a second toy. Which should usually come first?
Options
Safe school shoes
A second toy
Whichever costs more
Correct Answer:
Safe school shoes
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Sana needs safe shoes that fit for school and daily use. A second toy is optional and can wait.
Incorrect Feedback — A second toy:
A toy can be enjoyable, but safe shoes are more essential in this situation.
Incorrect Feedback — Whichever costs more:
A higher price does not automatically make an item more important.
Misconception Being Tested:
An expensive item must be more important than a cheaper one.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
Bilal really wants a game pass and thinks about it all day. Does that make it a need?
Options
No, wanting it strongly does not make it essential
Yes, because he wants it badly
Yes, if it is expensive
Correct Answer:
No, wanting it strongly does not make it essential.
Correct-Answer Explanation:
The game pass may be enjoyable, but it remains optional. Feelings can be strong without changing a want into a need.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because he wants it badly:
Wanting something strongly does not make it essential for health, safety, education, or daily living.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, if it is expensive:
Price does not decide whether something is a need.
Misconception Being Tested:
Every item a person strongly desires is a need.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
You forgot your lunch and have enough pocket money for either lunch or decorative stickers. What should usually come first?
Options
Lunch
Decorative stickers
Neither, because wants are bad
Correct Answer:
Lunch
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Lunch supports health during the school day, while decorative stickers are optional. The stickers can be considered after the need is covered.
Incorrect Feedback — Decorative stickers:
The stickers can be fun, but they can wait until the immediate food need is covered.
Incorrect Feedback — Neither, because wants are bad:
Wants are not bad. The goal is to cover the need first, then choose wants when possible.
Misconception Being Tested:
Wants are always bad or wasteful.
