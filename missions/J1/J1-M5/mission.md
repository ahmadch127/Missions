Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M5
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: Spending Wisely
Core Concept: Thoughtful spending involves pausing, checking your budget, and deciding whether a purchase is useful and affordable.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Recognise the difference between thoughtful spending decisions and impulsive purchases.
Primary Misconception:
Buying something on sale always saves money.
Secondary Misconceptions:
A limited-time offer must be accepted immediately.
A discounted item is a good purchase even when it was not planned or needed.
Key Takeaway:
A discount does not make an unnecessary purchase a good decision.

Screen Title: Before the Timer Ends
Question:
What should Maya do first?
Option 1
Answer: Check her budget
Recommended Answer: Yes
Response:
Good thinking. Maya can see whether the purchase fits without taking money from something already planned.
Option 2
Answer: Buy it immediately
Response:
She may get the art set, but the timer could push her into spending before thinking.
Option 3
Answer: Trust the sale label
Response:
A sale label shows a lower advertised price, but it does not prove the item is useful, affordable, or the cheapest available.
Option 4
Answer: Ignore all sales forever
Response:
Sales are not automatically bad. A discounted purchase can make sense when it is useful, planned, and affordable.

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Predict Outcome
Interaction Title: What Happens Next?
Instruction:
Explore each action and predict its likely outcome. Then choose Maya’s best first step.
Scenario
Maya has:
£25 total birthday money
£15 planned for school supplies
£10 available for fun
An unplanned art set costing £12
Ten minutes remaining on the advertised sale
Educational Assumptions
Maya still needs the full £15 for school supplies.
She has not compared the art set with prices at other shops.
The sale might end, but the item might also remain available later.
The activity shows possible outcomes, not guaranteed results.
Prediction 1 — Buy Immediately
Action:
Maya buys the art set before the timer ends.
Outcome Options
She gets the set but uses £2 planned for school supplies.
Buying the sale item adds £8 to her money.
Likely Outcome:
She gets the set but uses £2 planned for school supplies.
Explanation:
The art set costs £12, but Maya has only £10 available for fun. Buying it would change her school-supplies plan.

Prediction 2 — Wait One Day
Action:
Maya closes the sale and waits until tomorrow.
Outcome Options
The sale may end, but she gets time to decide without the countdown pressure.
The shop must keep the discount for her.
Likely Outcome:
The sale may end, but she gets time to decide without the countdown pressure.
Explanation:
Waiting does not guarantee that the price will remain. It can help Maya discover whether she still wants the item after the urgency fades.

Prediction 3 — Compare Prices
Action:
Maya checks the prices of similar art sets elsewhere.
Outcome Options
She may find a better price or learn that the discount is not unusual.
The original sale label proves it is the cheapest option.
Likely Outcome:
She may find a better price or learn that the discount is not unusual.
Explanation:
Comparing prices gives Maya more information. A crossed-out price alone does not show whether the current offer is the best available.

Prediction 4 — Check the Budget
Action:
Maya checks her school-supplies and fun-money envelopes.
Outcome Options
She sees that £12 does not fit her £10 fun budget without changing another plan.
The discount automatically makes the purchase affordable.
Likely Outcome:
She sees that £12 does not fit her £10 fun budget without changing another plan.
Explanation:
A lower price can still be unaffordable. Checking the budget shows the effect of the purchase on Maya’s other goals.
Optional Confidence Selection
Before checking each prediction, the learner may select:

Very sure

Recommended Completion State
The learner correctly predicts all four likely outcomes and selects:

Why “Check the Budget” Comes First
Checking the budget quickly reveals that the art set costs £2 more than Maya’s available fun money. She can then compare prices or wait without risking money already planned for school supplies.
Validation Method
Check each predicted outcome after submission.
Accept only the stated likely outcome for each action.
Complete the interaction after all four outcomes are explored and the best first step is selected.
Success Feedback
Great prediction. You looked past the timer and checked what each choice could do to Maya’s money plan.
Incorrect Feedback
Take another look at Maya’s numbers. Ask what the choice could change, not just what the sale promises.
Incomplete Feedback
Explore all four actions and choose Maya’s best first step before finishing.
Retry Behaviour
Keep correct predictions marked. Return only the incorrect prediction for another attempt and provide one short hint.
Accessibility Alternative
Present each action and its two possible outcomes as numbered radio-button choices. Do not require dragging, timed input, or animations.
Screen-reader instruction:
Choose an action, select its likely outcome, and submit. Repeat until all four actions have been explored.
Primary Button Label: Check My Predictions

6. Concept Reveal
Reveal Title: Pause Before You Purchase
Explanation:
You predicted what could happen after each choice. Thoughtful spending means pausing, checking your budget, and deciding whether a purchase is useful and affordable. A sale lowers the price, but spending £12 still costs £12. Checking the budget protects money already planned for school supplies. Comparing prices tests whether the deal is truly good. Waiting can reduce pressure and show whether you still want the item later. A discount helps only when the purchase already makes sense.
Suggested Visual:
A pause button stands between the sale timer and Maya’s wallet. Three question cards read: Is it useful?, Can I afford it?, and Is this a good price?
Explain It Simply:
Pause, check your plan, and then decide.
Primary Button Label: Try Another Situation

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Ella sees a £6 notebook on sale. She already has two unused notebooks. Which choice is most thoughtful?
Options
Pause and keep the money for her planned goal
Buy it because the price is lower
Buy two to save even more
Assume the sale ends forever
Correct Answer:
Pause and keep the money for her planned goal
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Ella does not currently need another notebook. Skipping an unnecessary purchase keeps the full £6 available for something she has planned.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy it because the price is lower:
The notebook costs less than before, but Ella would still spend £6 on something she does not currently need.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy two to save even more:
Buying more increases the total amount spent. It does not create savings when the notebooks were not needed.
Incorrect Feedback — Assume the sale ends forever:
The offer may end, but urgency alone does not make buying the notebook a thoughtful choice.
Misconception Being Tested:
A discounted item is a good purchase even when it was not planned or needed.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
A toy drops from £20 to £12. You did not plan to buy it and have only £5 available. Does buying it save you money?
Options
No, it makes you spend £12 you had not planned
Yes, it always saves £8
Yes, because every sale is a bargain
Yes, if the timer is nearly finished
Correct Answer:
No, it makes you spend £12 you had not planned
Correct-Answer Explanation:
The price is £8 lower, but buying the toy still requires spending £12. It also does not fit the £5 available.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, it always saves £8:
The price reduction is £8, but you only keep that £8 by not buying at the original price. You still spend £12.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because every sale is a bargain:
A sale can be useful, but only when the item makes sense for your needs and budget.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, if the timer is nearly finished:
A countdown creates urgency. It does not make the item affordable or necessary.
Misconception Being Tested:
Buying something on sale always saves money.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
Sam has £18. He needs £10 for a school trip and has £8 for fun. A £7 game appears in a flash sale. What should he do first?
Options
Check his budget and decide whether the game is useful
Buy immediately because time is short
Spend the school-trip money
Assume the sale price is the cheapest
Correct Answer:
Check his budget and decide whether the game is useful
Correct-Answer Explanation:
The game fits Sam’s fun-money amount, but he should still check that he wants it and that the purchase will not affect his school-trip money.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy immediately because time is short:
The countdown should not make the decision. Sam can pause long enough to check his plan.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend the school-trip money:
The school-trip money already has an important job and does not need to be used for the game.
Incorrect Feedback — Assume the sale price is the cheapest:
The sale might be good, but Sam would need to compare prices to know whether it is the best offer.
Misconception Being Tested:
A limited-time offer must be accepted immediately.
