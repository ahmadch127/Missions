Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M1
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: What Is Money?
Core Concept: Money is a commonly accepted medium of exchange that makes trading goods and services easier.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Understand why money exists and how it makes exchanging goods and services easier.
Primary Misconception:
Money automatically creates wealth.
Secondary Misconceptions:
Bartering always results in a fair and successful trade.
Money is useful only because coins and banknotes have physical value.
Key Takeaway:
Money makes exchange easier because people agree to accept it.

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Cause & Effect
Interaction Title: Build the Trade Chain
Instruction:
Arrange the four events from the problem to the result.
Events
People want to trade.
They cannot agree on what to exchange.
They use money.
Trading becomes easier.
Cause-and-Effect Relationships
Relationship 1
Cause: People want to trade.
Effect: They cannot agree on what to exchange.
Explanation:
Wanting to trade is not enough when each person wants something different.
Relationship 2
Cause: They cannot agree on what to exchange.
Effect: They use money.
Explanation:
Money gives them something commonly accepted to exchange.
Relationship 3
Cause: They use money.
Effect: Trading becomes easier.
Explanation:
Each person can accept money and then use it in another trade.
Correct Completion State
People want to trade.
They cannot agree on what to exchange.
They use money.
Trading becomes easier.
Alternative Contributing Factors
Both people still need to agree on the amount.
They need to trust that other people will also accept the money.
The item or service must still be available.
Explanation of Simplification
Money usually makes exchange easier, but it does not guarantee a fair price or a successful deal.
Validation Method
Check the full order after the learner submits. Accept only the defined sequence.
Success Feedback
That chain works. Money connects people who want different things, so the exchange is easier.
Incorrect Feedback
Almost. Start with the reason people need a trade, then find what solves the stuck exchange.
Incomplete Feedback
Place all four events before checking the chain.
Retry Behaviour
Keep correctly placed events in position, gently highlight the first misplaced event, and allow unlimited retries.
Accessibility Alternative
Provide numbered selection controls and Move Up or Move Down buttons instead of requiring drag gestures.
Screen-reader instruction:
Choose an event, assign its position from 1 to 4, then submit the sequence.
Primary Button Label: Check the Chain

6. Concept Reveal
Reveal Title: Money Connects Different Wants
Explanation:
You arranged a chain: people wanted to trade, the direct swap failed, they used money, and trading became easier. Money is a commonly accepted medium of exchange. People accept it when buying and selling, even when they do not want each other’s items. A £20 note is useful mainly because people trust others will accept it—not because its paper is worth £20. Money helps exchange value; it does not automatically create wealth or guarantee a fair price.
Suggested Visual:
A £20 note forms a bridge between a cricket bat and a bicycle, with arrows showing two separate exchanges.
Explain It Simply:
Money works because people agree to accept it for exchange.
Primary Button Label: Try Another Example

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Mia sells a puzzle for £4, then uses the £4 to buy pencils. What made both exchanges easier?
Options
Both sellers accepted the £4
The puzzle became money
The pencils were free
Mia became automatically wealthy
Correct Answer:
Both sellers accepted the £4.
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Money worked as a shared way to pay. The puzzle buyer and pencil seller both accepted £4, even though they wanted different items.
Incorrect Feedback — The puzzle became money:
The puzzle stayed a puzzle. Mia exchanged it for money that another seller accepted.
Incorrect Feedback — The pencils were free:
Mia paid £4 for the pencils, so they were not free.
Incorrect Feedback — Mia became automatically wealthy:
Money helped Mia exchange value, but one trade did not automatically make her wealthy.
Misconception Being Tested:
Money is useful only because coins and banknotes have physical value.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
A pretend town prints twice as much money but makes no extra food, bicycles, or services. Is it automatically twice as wealthy?
Options
Yes, twice as wealthy
No, extra money alone adds no goods or services
Yes, because notes have physical value
Correct Answer:
No, extra money alone adds no goods or services.
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Money helps people exchange and measure value. Simply creating more money does not automatically create more useful goods, services, skills, or resources.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, twice as wealthy:
More money by itself does not create extra useful goods or services.
Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because notes have physical value:
The paper itself is not what makes a note worth its printed amount. Shared acceptance gives it its main use.
Misconception Being Tested:
Money automatically creates wealth.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
Zara wants Noah’s comic. Noah does not want Zara’s stickers, but he will accept £3. What could Zara do?
Options
Find a sticker buyer, receive £3, then buy the comic
Force Noah to swap
Say the stickers and £3 are identical
Take the comic without agreement
Correct Answer:
Find a sticker buyer, receive £3, then buy the comic.
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Zara can exchange the stickers with someone who wants them, then use commonly accepted money to buy the comic from Noah.
Incorrect Feedback — Force Noah to swap:
A trade needs agreement. Money can help, but it cannot replace consent.
Incorrect Feedback — Say the stickers and £3 are identical:
They are different things. The useful point is that Noah agrees to accept the £3.
Incorrect Feedback — Take the comic without agreement:
That is not a trade. Both people must agree before an exchange happens.
Misconception Being Tested:
Bartering always results in a fair and successful trade.
