Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M4
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: Saving First
Core Concept: Saving is more effective when a specific amount is set aside before money is spent on optional items.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Understand why saving should be planned and included in a budget before other spending.
Primary Misconception:
Saving means keeping whatever money remains at the end of the month.
Secondary Misconceptions:
You can only save when you receive a large amount of money.
Spending on wants first will not affect a savings goal.
Key Takeaway:
Saving works better when it is planned before spending.

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Budget Builder
Interaction Title: Give Every Rupee a Job
Instruction:
Divide Rs. 3,000 between saving, transport, snacks, and entertainment. Watch how your saving choice changes the headphones finish date.
Educational Scenario Assumptions
You receive Rs. 3,000 at the start of each month.
School transport costs at least Rs. 800 each month.
The headphones continue to cost Rs. 12,000.
You begin with Rs. 0 saved.
You save the same selected amount every month.
No extra money is added and no money is removed from the saving jar.
These amounts and limits apply only to this learning activity.
Total Available Amount
Rs. 3,000 per month
Saving Goal
Rs. 12,000 for headphones
Allocation Step
Amounts change in steps of Rs. 100.
Budget Categories
Saving
Purpose: Money set aside for the headphones goal
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 2,200
Required for Mission Completion: At least Rs. 100
Transport
Purpose: Required school transport
Minimum: Rs. 800
Maximum: Rs. 1,500
Required Expense: Yes
Snacks
Purpose: Optional snacks and treats
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 1,000
Required Expense: No
Entertainment
Purpose: Optional games, videos, or other entertainment
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 1,200
Required Expense: No
Remaining Balance
The remaining balance equals Rs. 3,000 minus all four allocations.
A completed budget must have a remaining balance of Rs. 0.
Goal-Time Display
The learner sees an estimated finish time based on the monthly saving amount.
Examples
Rs. 0 saved monthly: No finish date under this plan
Rs. 500 saved monthly: 24 months
Rs. 1,000 saved monthly: 12 months
Rs. 1,500 saved monthly: 8 months
Rs. 2,000 saved monthly: 6 months
When the result is not a whole number, round up to the next full month because the full Rs. 12,000 is still needed.
Immediate Consequences
When Saving Increases
The estimated finish date moves closer, while less money remains for optional spending.
When Snacks or Entertainment Increase
The amount available for saving may fall, and the estimated finish date may move further away.
When Saving Is Rs. 0
The display reads:
“Your goal is waiting. This budget does not move the saving jar forward.”
When Transport Is Below Rs. 800
The display reads:
“Transport needs at least Rs. 800 in this story.”
Valid Completion Rules
A budget is valid when:
All four categories stay within their allowed ranges.
Transport receives at least Rs. 800.
Saving receives at least Rs. 100.
The four allocations total exactly Rs. 3,000.
The remaining balance is Rs. 0.
Recommended Example Completion
This is one balanced example, not the only correct answer:
Saving: Rs. 1,000
Transport: Rs. 800
Snacks: Rs. 600
Entertainment: Rs. 600
Remaining balance: Rs. 0
Estimated goal time: 12 months
Multiple Valid Budgets
Multiple arrangements are valid. Learners may choose different amounts for snacks and entertainment as long as transport is covered, saving is planned, and the total remains Rs. 3,000.
A larger monthly saving reaches the goal sooner, but the mission does not describe all optional spending as irresponsible.
Validation Method
Check category limits, required transport, planned saving, total allocation, and remaining balance when the learner submits.
Success Feedback
Your budget gives saving a place before optional spending. At this pace, the finish date is part of the plan—not a leftover surprise.
Dynamic Success Message
“You planned to save Rs. [monthly saving] each month. At that pace, you could reach Rs. 12,000 in about [goal months] months.”
Incorrect Feedback
Your budget needs another look. Cover transport, include a planned saving amount, and make sure the total equals Rs. 3,000.
Incomplete Feedback
Some money still needs a job. Allocate the full Rs. 3,000 before checking your budget.
Retry Behaviour
Keep valid category amounts in place. Highlight only the condition that needs attention and allow unlimited adjustments.
Accessibility Alternative
Provide plus and minus buttons, number inputs, or selectable amount lists for each category. Do not require sliders or dragging.
Screen-reader instruction:
Choose an amount for each category. Listen to the remaining balance and estimated goal time, then submit when the total is Rs. 3,000.
Primary Button Label: Check My Budget

6. Concept Reveal
Reveal Title: Save Before the Money Disappears
Explanation:
You watched the finish date change as you moved money between categories. This is planned saving, often called paying yourself first: choose a saving amount before optional spending begins. Even a small regular amount can move a goal forward. If you buy wants first and save only leftovers, the amount may shrink or disappear. Saving first does not mean avoiding all fun. It means giving your goal a place in the budget before choosing snacks or entertainment.
Suggested Visual:
Coins enter a saving jar first. The remaining coins then split between transport, snacks, and entertainment.
Explain It Simply:
Put savings in the plan before spending on wants.
Primary Button Label: See Another Example

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Ayesha receives Rs. 800. Which choice shows planned saving?
Options
Put Rs. 100 in her saving jar before buying treats
Buy treats first and save anything left
Wait until she receives several thousand rupees
Spend it all because Rs. 800 is too small
Correct Answer:
Put Rs. 100 in her saving jar before buying treats
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Ayesha chooses a saving amount before optional spending. A small planned amount still moves her goal forward.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy treats first and save anything left:
Saving leftovers is uncertain because the treats may use all the available money.
Incorrect Feedback — Wait until she receives several thousand rupees:
She does not need to wait for a large amount. Smaller regular savings can also build over time.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend it all because Rs. 800 is too small:
A small amount can still be divided. Even Rs. 100 would begin moving the goal forward.
Misconception Being Tested:
You can only save when you receive a large amount of money.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
Hamza spends throughout the month and plans to save whatever remains. Some months, nothing remains. What would help most?
Options
Choose a saving amount at the start
Keep hoping more money remains
Buy more wants early
Stop having any fun forever
Correct Answer:
Choose a saving amount at the start
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Planning the saving amount first gives the goal a regular place in Hamza’s budget instead of depending on uncertain leftovers.
Incorrect Feedback — Keep hoping more money remains:
Hope does not reserve money. Optional spending may still use the full amount.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy more wants early:
More early spending would leave even less money for the saving goal.
Incorrect Feedback — Stop having any fun forever:
Saving first does not require removing every want. Hamza can plan both saving and some enjoyment.
Misconception Being Tested:
Saving means keeping whatever money remains at the end of the month.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
You receive Rs. 2,000, need Rs. 700 for transport, and want to save for a school bag. Which plan uses saving first?
Options
Save Rs. 500, cover transport, then plan the remaining Rs. 800
Spend Rs. 800 on games before planning
Save only if money remains later
Use the transport money for entertainment
Correct Answer:
Save Rs. 500, cover transport, then plan the remaining Rs. 800
Correct-Answer Explanation:
The plan includes both the saving goal and required transport before optional spending.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend Rs. 800 on games before planning:
Spending on the want first could reduce the amount available for saving or transport.
Incorrect Feedback — Save only if money remains later:
That makes saving uncertain. A planned amount gives the goal a clear place in the budget.
Incorrect Feedback — Use the transport money for entertainment:
Transport is required in this situation, so it should be covered before entertainment.
Misconception Being Tested:
Spending on wants first will not affect a savings goal.
