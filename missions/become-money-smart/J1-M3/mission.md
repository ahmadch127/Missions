Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M3
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: How Money Is Earned
Core Concept: People usually earn money by doing work, selling useful products, or providing services that create value for others.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Understand the different ways people can earn money by creating value for someone else.
Primary Misconception:
Money only comes from salaries or parents.
Secondary Misconceptions:
A person must have a formal job to earn money.
Waiting for someone to give you money is the same as earning it.
Key Takeaway:
Money is usually earned by creating value for someone.

4. Pre-Interaction Question
Screen Title: What Makes It Earning?
Question:
Which clue best shows that money was earned?
Option 1
Answer: Someone received useful value in return
Recommended Answer: Yes
Response:
Good thinking. Earning usually involves providing work, a product, or a service that someone values.
Option 2
Answer: The money came from an adult
Response:
Adults can pay for work, but they can also give gifts or pocket money.
Option 3
Answer: The money was given as coins
Response:
The form of the money does not show whether it was earned.
Option 4
Answer: The person waited long enough
Response:
Waiting may lead to receiving money, but it does not automatically mean the money was earned.
Primary Button Label: Test My Idea

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Decision Tree
Interaction Title: Choose Ayaan’s Money Path
Instruction:
Explore all four choices. Follow each path and decide whether Ayaan earns money or simply receives it.
Starting Node
Ayaan wants to collect £12 for a sketchbook. An adult has approved three safe, age-appropriate ways he could try to earn part of the money.
Choice 1 — Help at the Family Shop
Choice:
Help organise greeting cards at the family shop.
Consequence:
Ayaan spends one hour sorting the cards. The organised display saves the shopkeeper time. The family agrees to pay him £4.
Follow-Up Node:
What did Ayaan provide?
Follow-Up Choices
Useful work
Nothing except waiting
Recommended Follow-Up Choice:
Useful work
Terminal Outcome:
Earned through work: Ayaan created value by completing a useful task and earned £4.

Choice 2 — Sell Drawings
Choice:
Offer two original drawings for sale with an adult’s permission.
Consequence:
Two people enjoy the drawings and agree to buy them for £3 each. Ayaan receives £6.
Follow-Up Node:
What did Ayaan offer?
Follow-Up Choices
A product people wanted
A monthly salary
Recommended Follow-Up Choice:
A product people wanted
Terminal Outcome:
Earned by selling a product: Ayaan created something people valued and earned £6.

Choice 3 — Tutor Another Student
Choice:
Help a younger student practise multiplication with both families’ permission.
Consequence:
Ayaan explains the questions clearly for 30 minutes. The other student improves, and the family pays Ayaan £5.
Follow-Up Node:
What did Ayaan provide?
Follow-Up Choices
A helpful service
A physical product
Recommended Follow-Up Choice:
A helpful service
Terminal Outcome:
Earned by providing a service: Ayaan used his time and skill to help someone and earned £5.

Choice 4 — Wait for Pocket Money
Choice:
Wait until his family’s next pocket-money day.
Consequence:
Ayaan receives £3 from his family. In this path, he did not complete an agreed task, sell a product, or provide a service.
Follow-Up Node:
Did Ayaan directly earn this £3?
Follow-Up Choices
Yes, because he received money
No, it was received rather than directly earned
Recommended Follow-Up Choice:
No, it was received rather than directly earned
Terminal Outcome:
Money received: The £3 still helps Ayaan, but this particular payment was given rather than directly earned.

Correct Completion State
The learner explores all four branches and identifies:
Help at the family shop: Earned through work
Sell drawings: Earned by selling a product
Tutor another student: Earned by providing a service
Wait for pocket money: Received without directly earning it
Validation Method
Each branch is checked after its follow-up choice. The interaction is complete when all four paths have been explored and correctly identified.
Success Feedback
Great exploring. Ayaan can earn money through work, products, or services. Pocket money can be useful, but receiving it is not always the same as earning it.
Incorrect Feedback
Look for the exchange. Did Ayaan provide useful work, a product, or a service in return for the money?
Incomplete Feedback
Explore every path before finishing the decision tree.
Retry Behaviour
Keep completed branches marked. Return the learner to the current follow-up node and provide one short hint. Allow unlimited retries.
Accessibility Alternative
Present the tree as a numbered list of four choices. Each branch opens as a linear text sequence with selectable buttons.
Screen-reader instruction:
Choose a path, listen to its consequence, identify how the money arrived, and return to explore the remaining paths.
Trade-Off Summary
Work: Requires time and effort, and the task must be safe and useful.
Product: Requires something another person wants and may require materials.
Service: Requires time, skill, and someone who needs the help.
Pocket money: Depends on the giver and may be received without creating value in return.
There is no single best earning path. Available choices depend on skills, permission, safety, time, and what other people value.
Primary Button Label: Finish the Tree

6. Concept Reveal
Reveal Title: Creating Value Can Earn Money
Explanation:
You explored four paths. Shop work created value through a useful task. Drawings created value as products. Tutoring created value as a service. These are ways of earning money: receiving payment because someone values what you did or provided. Waiting for pocket money may still bring money, but it is received rather than directly earned. A salary is one way to earn, not the only way. Children’s activities should always be safe and approved by an adult.
Suggested Visual:
Three arrows labelled Work, Product, and Service lead toward an earning jar. A separate Money Received arrow leads from a gift box.
Explain It Simply:
You earn by offering work, products, or services people value.

7. Real-Life Example
Title: The Plant-Watering Helper
With a parent’s permission, Lily waters a neighbour’s plants while the neighbour is away. The neighbour agrees to pay her £5. Lily does not have a formal job or salary, but she earns money by providing a useful service.
Suggested Visual:
Lily waters several plants while a parent watches nearby. A neighbour thanks her and places £5 in her saving jar.

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Theo helps organise books for a school fair and receives an agreed payment of £4. How did he earn it?
Options
By doing useful work
By waiting for money
By receiving a birthday gift
By borrowing it
Correct Answer:
By doing useful work
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Theo completed a useful task that helped prepare the school fair. The agreed payment was earned through work.
Incorrect Feedback — By waiting for money:
Theo did more than wait. He completed a useful task in return for the payment.
Incorrect Feedback — By receiving a birthday gift:
The £4 was connected to the work he completed, so it was not a birthday gift.
Incorrect Feedback — By borrowing it:
Borrowed money must usually be returned. Theo received payment for his work.
Misconception Being Tested:
Waiting for someone to give you money is the same as earning it.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
Which statement about earning money is correct?
Options
People can earn through work, products, or services
Only people with salaries can earn money
Money only comes from parents
Every gift is earned money
Correct Answer:
People can earn through work, products, or services
Correct-Answer Explanation:
A salary is one form of earning, but people can also earn by completing useful work, selling products, or providing services.
Incorrect Feedback — Only people with salaries can earn money:
A formal salary is not required. A person may earn from a useful task, product, or service.
Incorrect Feedback — Money only comes from parents:
Parents may give money, but people can also earn it by creating value for others.
Incorrect Feedback — Every gift is earned money:
A gift can be received without work, a product, or a service being provided in return.
Misconception Being Tested:
Money only comes from salaries or parents.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
Amelia wants to earn £8. Which choice directly creates value for someone else?
Options
Offer to organise craft supplies for an agreed payment
Wait for birthday money
Ask to be given £8
Look at the saving jar each day
Correct Answer:
Offer to organise craft supplies for an agreed payment
Correct-Answer Explanation:
Organising the supplies is useful work. If an adult agrees to pay for the task, Amelia earns money by creating value.
Incorrect Feedback — Wait for birthday money:
Birthday money may be received, but waiting for it is not directly earning it.
Incorrect Feedback — Ask to be given £8:
Receiving money after asking is different from earning it through work, a product, or a service.
Incorrect Feedback — Look at the saving jar each day:
Watching the jar does not create value or increase the money inside it.
Misconception Being Tested:
A person must have a formal job to earn money.
