Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document
1. Mission Metadata
Mission ID: J1-M6
Journey: Become Money Smart
Mission Title: Build a Budget
Core Concept: A budget is a plan that helps divide limited money between essential expenses, savings, emergencies, and enjoyable activities.
Target Age Group: Kids 8–12
Difficulty: Beginner
Estimated Duration: 4 minutes
Currency and Locale: Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
Language: English
Learning Objective:
Learn how to divide a limited amount of money across different priorities so it lasts for the entire month.
Primary Misconception:
Budgeting means removing all fun and spending only on essential needs.
Secondary Misconceptions:
A budget is useful only when someone has very little money.
Emergency money and savings are unnecessary when all current expenses are covered.
Key Takeaway:
A budget is a plan for using money, not a punishment.

5. Main Interaction
Interaction Type: Budget Builder
Interaction Title: Build a 30-Day Budget
Instruction:
Divide Rs. 20,000 across all six categories. Cover the essentials, prepare for later, and avoid spending more than you have.
Educational Scenario Assumptions
For this activity:
The Rs. 20,000 must last for 30 days.
Food, transport, and mobile data are required in this situation.
The listed minimums are simplified learning amounts, not real prices for every family.
Savings are for a future goal.
Emergency money is kept available for an unexpected need.
Entertainment is optional, but including affordable fun is allowed.
Total Available Amount
Rs. 20,000
Allocation Step
Amounts change in steps of Rs. 500.
Budget Categories
Food
Purpose: Meals and basic groceries
Minimum: Rs. 7,000
Maximum: Rs. 10,000
Required Expense: Yes
Transport
Purpose: Necessary travel during the month
Minimum: Rs. 2,500
Maximum: Rs. 5,000
Required Expense: Yes
Mobile Data
Purpose: Required calls, messages, and online access
Minimum: Rs. 1,000
Maximum: Rs. 2,500
Required Expense: Yes
Savings
Purpose: Money kept for a future goal
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 5,000
Minimum for Mission Success: Rs. 1,000
Entertainment
Purpose: Games, outings, or other enjoyable activities
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 4,000
Required Expense: No
Emergency Money
Purpose: Money available for an unexpected need
Minimum: Rs. 0
Maximum: Rs. 4,000
Minimum for Mission Success: Rs. 1,000
Remaining Balance
The remaining balance equals:
Rs. 20,000 minus all six allocations
A completed budget must have a remaining balance of Rs. 0.
The learner cannot allocate more than Rs. 20,000.
Immediate Feedback
Food Below Rs. 7,000
Message:
“Food needs at least Rs. 7,000 in this activity.”
Transport Below Rs. 2,500
Message:
“Transport is not fully covered yet.”
Mobile Data Below Rs. 1,000
Message:
“Set aside enough for the required mobile data.”
Savings Below Rs. 1,000
Message:
“Your current costs are covered, but your future goal needs a place too.”
Emergency Money Below Rs. 1,000
Message:
“A surprise expense could disrupt this plan. Add some emergency money.”
Spending Above Rs. 20,000
Message:
“This plan spends more money than is available. Reduce one or more categories.”
Entertainment Above Rs. 0
Message:
“You included affordable fun while staying within the plan.”
Entertainment at Rs. 0
Message:
“This plan can work, but remember that a budget may include affordable fun if you choose.”
Valid Completion Rules
A budget is valid when:
Food receives at least Rs. 7,000.
Transport receives at least Rs. 2,500.
Mobile data receives at least Rs. 1,000.
Savings receives at least Rs. 1,000.
Emergency money receives at least Rs. 1,000.
Every category stays within its allowed range.
The allocations total exactly Rs. 20,000.
The remaining balance is Rs. 0.
Recommended Example Completion
This is one balanced example, not the only correct plan:
Food: Rs. 8,000
Transport: Rs. 3,000
Mobile data: Rs. 1,500
Savings: Rs. 3,000
Entertainment: Rs. 2,000
Emergency money: Rs. 2,500
Remaining balance: Rs. 0
Multiple Valid Budgets
Many budgets can succeed. Learners may move money between categories within the allowed limits.
There is no single perfect entertainment amount. A learner may choose more affordable fun or place more money in savings or emergencies.
Scoring Rules
Essentials Covered — 40 Points
Awarded when food, transport, and mobile data meet their minimums.
Future Goal Included — 20 Points
Awarded when savings receives at least Rs. 1,000.
Surprise Protection Included — 20 Points
Awarded when emergency money receives at least Rs. 1,000.
No Overspending — 20 Points
Awarded when the allocations total exactly Rs. 20,000.
Mission Success Score: 100 points
Entertainment does not reduce the score when it remains within the allowed budget.
Correct or Recommended Completion State
Any valid 100-point budget is accepted.
The recommended example demonstrates that essentials, saving, emergency preparation, and entertainment can all fit in one plan.
Validation Method
Check category limits, required minimums, total allocation, remaining balance, and score when the learner submits.
Success Feedback
Budget balanced! You covered important costs, prepared for the future and surprises, and stayed within Rs. 20,000.
Dynamic Success Feedback
When entertainment is included:
“You also made room for Rs. [entertainment amount] of affordable fun.”
When entertainment is Rs. 0:
“Your plan works. You could also include affordable fun by adjusting another flexible category.”
Incorrect Feedback
Your budget needs another look. Check the highlighted category and make sure the full plan stays within Rs. 20,000.
Incomplete Feedback
Some money still needs a job. Allocate the full Rs. 20,000 before checking your budget.
Retry Behaviour
Keep valid amounts in place. Highlight only unmet conditions and allow unlimited adjustments.
Accessibility Alternative
Provide plus and minus buttons, number inputs, or selectable amounts for every category. Do not require sliders or dragging.
Screen-reader instruction:
Choose an amount for each category. Listen to the remaining balance and status messages, then submit when all Rs. 20,000 has been allocated.
Primary Button Label: Check My Budget

You divided limited money between today’s needs, future goals, surprises, and fun. That plan is called a budget. A budget helps you decide where money should go before it disappears. Entertainment can also belong in a budget when important costs are covered and the total stays affordable.

8. Quiz
Question 1 — Recognition
Question:
Which plan is a complete budget for Rs. 5,000?
Options
Plan needs, saving, emergency money, and affordable fun
Spend until the money runs out
Buy fun items and ignore the total
Keep every rupee unused
Correct Answer:
Plan needs, saving, emergency money, and affordable fun
Correct-Answer Explanation:
A complete budget gives different priorities planned amounts while keeping total spending within Rs. 5,000.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend until the money runs out:
Spending without a plan may leave important costs uncovered before the money period ends.
Incorrect Feedback — Buy fun items and ignore the total:
Fun can be included, but the full plan still needs to stay within the available amount.
Incorrect Feedback — Keep every rupee unused:
A budget helps money cover useful priorities. It does not require avoiding all spending.
Misconception Being Tested:
A budget is only about restricting spending.

Question 2 — Misconception
Question:
Which Rs. 10,000 plan shows that budgeting can include fun?
Options
Rs. 6,500 needs, Rs. 1,500 saving, Rs. 1,000 emergency, Rs. 1,000 fun
Rs. 10,000 entertainment
Rs. 10,000 needs and nothing else
Spend without choosing amounts
Correct Answer:
Rs. 6,500 needs, Rs. 1,500 saving, Rs. 1,000 emergency, Rs. 1,000 fun
Correct-Answer Explanation:
This plan covers important costs, prepares for later, includes enjoyable spending, and stays within Rs. 10,000.
Incorrect Feedback — Rs. 10,000 entertainment:
Entertainment can belong in a budget, but using the full amount would leave other priorities uncovered.
Incorrect Feedback — Rs. 10,000 needs and nothing else:
Needs matter, but budgeting can also prepare for future goals, surprises, and affordable enjoyment.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend without choosing amounts:
Without planned amounts, it is harder to know whether the money will cover every priority.
Misconception Being Tested:
Budgeting means removing all fun.

Question 3 — Application
Question:
Ali has Rs. 6,000. His required costs are Rs. 4,000. Which plan is strongest?
Options
Cover Rs. 4,000, save Rs. 1,000, keep Rs. 500 for emergencies, and use Rs. 500 for fun
Spend Rs. 2,000 on games first
Use all Rs. 6,000 on current costs
Ignore emergencies because nothing is wrong today
Correct Answer:
Cover Rs. 4,000, save Rs. 1,000, keep Rs. 500 for emergencies, and use Rs. 500 for fun
Correct-Answer Explanation:
The plan covers current costs, prepares for a future goal and unexpected needs, includes fun, and uses exactly Rs. 6,000.
Incorrect Feedback — Spend Rs. 2,000 on games first:
That may leave too little for the Rs. 4,000 of required costs.
Incorrect Feedback — Use all Rs. 6,000 on current costs:
Current costs would be covered, but no money would be prepared for future goals or surprises.
Incorrect Feedback — Ignore emergencies because nothing is wrong today:
Emergency money is prepared before something unexpected happens.
Misconception Being Tested:
Emergency money and savings are unnecessary when current expenses are covered.
