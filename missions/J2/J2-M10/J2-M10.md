# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J2-M10
**Journey:** Make Money Grow
**Mission Title:** Inflation
**Core Concept:** Inflation means prices increase over time, which can reduce how much a person can buy with the same amount of money.
**Target Age Group:** Kids 8–12
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how rising prices affect purchasing power, even when income also increases.

**Primary Misconception:**
A higher salary always means a person is becoming richer.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Having more money always means being able to buy more things.
* A small increase in income is enough to protect against any increase in prices.

**Key Takeaway:**
More money does not always mean more buying power.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Before vs After

**Interaction Title:** What Can the Money Buy?

**Instruction:**
Complete the before-and-after comparison. Then decide whether buying power increased or decreased.

## Educational Assumptions

* This is a simple learning example.
* All monthly income is compared with the price of one burger only.
* The activity does not suggest spending all income on burgers.
* The burger size and quality remain the same.
* No discounts, taxes, or other costs are included.
* One product becoming more expensive does not prove economy-wide inflation by itself.
* Inflation describes a general rise in prices across many goods and services over time.

---

## Before State

**Monthly income:** Rs. 20,000
**Burger price:** Rs. 500

### Learner Question

How many Rs. 500 burgers could Rs. 20,000 buy?

### Options

1. 20 burgers
2. 30 burgers
3. **40 burgers**
4. 50 burgers

### Calculation

**Rs. 20,000 ÷ Rs. 500 = 40 burgers**

### Before Result

**Buying power:** 40 burgers

---

## After State

**Monthly income:** Rs. 21,000
**Burger price:** Rs. 700

### Learner Question

How many Rs. 700 burgers could Rs. 21,000 buy?

### Options

1. 21 burgers
2. **30 burgers**
3. 40 burgers
4. 42 burgers

### Calculation

**Rs. 21,000 ÷ Rs. 700 = 30 burgers**

### After Result

**Buying power:** 30 burgers

---

## Values Being Compared

| Comparison         |     Before |      After |
| ------------------ | ---------: | ---------: |
| Monthly income     | Rs. 20,000 | Rs. 21,000 |
| Burger price       |    Rs. 500 |    Rs. 700 |
| Burgers affordable |         40 |         30 |

## Final Learner Question

Can the higher income buy more burgers than before?

### Options

1. Yes, because Rs. 21,000 is more money
2. **No, because the burger price rose faster**
3. Yes, because every income increase creates wealth
4. No, because income increases are always bad

**Correct Answer:**
No, because the burger price rose faster.

## Important Hidden Difference

The amount of money increased by Rs. 1,000, but the number of burgers it could buy fell by 10.

The income increased by **5%**, while the burger price increased by **40%** in this example.

## Purchasing-Power Change

* **Before:** 40 burgers
* **After:** 30 burgers
* **Change:** 10 fewer burgers
* **Result:** Purchasing power decreased

## Correct Completion State

The learner identifies:

* **Before buying power:** 40 burgers
* **After buying power:** 30 burgers
* **Change:** 10 fewer burgers
* **Income amount:** Higher
* **Purchasing power:** Lower
* **Reason:** The burger price rose faster than income

## Validation Method

Check the before calculation, after calculation, and final purchasing-power answer. Complete the interaction when all three are correct.

## Success Feedback

Excellent comparison. The money amount increased, but it could buy 10 fewer burgers because the price rose faster.

## Incorrect Feedback

Do not compare only the income numbers. Divide each income amount by the burger price for that time.

## Incomplete Feedback

Calculate both burger totals and decide what happened to buying power.

## Retry Behaviour

Keep correct calculations marked. Show the division needed for an incorrect answer and allow unlimited retries.

## Accessibility Alternative

Present each state as a linear card with the income, price, and radio-button answers. Read each calculation aloud.

**Screen-reader instruction:**
Listen to the income and burger price. Choose how many burgers can be bought, then compare the two results.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** When Prices Outrun Money

**Explanation:**
Your income rose from Rs. 20,000 to Rs. 21,000, but your burger buying power fell from 40 to 30. **Purchasing power** means how much your money can buy. **Inflation** is a general rise in prices across many goods and services over time. When prices rise faster than income, money may buy less even when the income number is higher. A bigger amount of money matters only when compared with what things cost.

**Suggested Visual:**
A larger Rs. 21,000 wallet sits on one side, while the burger stack shrinks from 40 to 30 on the other.

**Explain It Simply:**
If prices rise faster than income, your money buys less.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Last year, Rs. 600 bought three toys at Rs. 200 each. Now each toy costs Rs. 300. How many can Rs. 600 buy?

### Options

1. One toy
2. **Two toys**
3. Three toys
4. Four toys

**Correct Answer:**
Two toys

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Rs. 600 divided by Rs. 300 equals two. The same money now buys one fewer toy.

**Incorrect Feedback — One toy:**
One toy costs Rs. 300, so Rs. 600 can buy two.

**Incorrect Feedback — Three toys:**
Three toys would cost Rs. 900 at the new price.

**Incorrect Feedback — Four toys:**
Four toys would cost Rs. 1,200 at the new price.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Having the same amount of money always means being able to buy the same number of things.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
A person’s income rises from Rs. 30,000 to Rs. 31,000, but their usual costs rise from Rs. 25,000 to Rs. 28,000. Are they automatically better off?

### Options

1. **No, their costs rose by more than their income**
2. Yes, because any income increase creates wealth
3. Yes, because Rs. 31,000 is a larger number
4. No, because higher income is always harmful

**Correct Answer:**
No, their costs rose by more than their income.

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Income rose by Rs. 1,000, while usual costs rose by Rs. 3,000. The person has less money remaining after those costs.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because any income increase creates wealth:**
An income increase helps only when compared with how much prices and costs also changed.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because Rs. 31,000 is a larger number:**
The income number is larger, but the person’s buying power may still fall when costs rise faster.

**Incorrect Feedback — No, because higher income is always harmful:**
Higher income can help. The important question is how it compares with changing prices.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A higher salary always means a person is becoming richer.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
Your pocket money rises from Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 1,100. A game rises from Rs. 500 to Rs. 600. What happens to your buying power?

### Options

1. **It falls from two games to one**
2. It rises from two games to three
3. It stays at two games
4. It cannot be calculated

**Correct Answer:**
It falls from two games to one.

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Before, Rs. 1,000 bought two games. After, Rs. 1,100 can buy only one full Rs. 600 game.

**Incorrect Feedback — It rises from two games to three:**
Three games would cost Rs. 1,800 at the new price.

**Incorrect Feedback — It stays at two games:**
Two games now cost Rs. 1,200, which is more than Rs. 1,100.

**Incorrect Feedback — It cannot be calculated:**
Divide each pocket-money amount by the game price for that time.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A small increase in income is enough to protect against any increase in prices.
