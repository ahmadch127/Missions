# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J2-M9
**Journey:** Make Money Grow
**Mission Title:** Interest
**Core Concept:** Interest is an amount added to money over time. Savers may earn interest, while borrowers usually pay interest.
**Target Age Group:** Kids 8–12
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand that interest can increase savings balances and make borrowed money more expensive to repay.

**Primary Misconception:**
Interest is always free money.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Savers and borrowers experience interest in the same way.
* A borrower only needs to repay the original amount borrowed.

**Key Takeaway:**
Interest can help savers and cost borrowers.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Before vs After

**Interaction Title:** One Year Later

**Instruction:**
Complete both before-and-after comparisons. Then decide whether the added interest helps or costs each person.

## Educational Assumptions

For this activity:

* Both examples start at Rs. 10,000.
* Both use a simple 10% rate for one year.
* Ten per cent of Rs. 10,000 is Rs. 1,000.
* The activity uses the same rate only to make the comparison easy.
* No extra deposits, withdrawals, fees, missed payments, or compounding are included.
* Real savings and loan rates may differ.
* The example does not promise that a real bank will offer this return or loan rate.

---

## Comparison 1 — Saving Money

### Before State

**Person:** Sara
**Situation:** Saving money
**Starting balance:** Rs. 10,000
**Interest rate used in activity:** 10% for one year
**Interest amount:** Not shown yet

### Learner Question

What will Sara’s balance be after Rs. 1,000 of interest is added?

### Options

1. Rs. 9,000
2. Rs. 10,000
3. **Rs. 11,000**
4. Rs. 20,000

### After State

**Starting savings:** Rs. 10,000
**Interest added:** Rs. 1,000
**Final savings balance:** Rs. 11,000
**Effect:** Sara’s money increases by Rs. 1,000.

### Explanation

Sara earns interest in this activity. The added money increases the balance she owns.

## Comparison 2 — Borrowing Money

### Before State

**Person:** Hamza
**Situation:** Borrowing money
**Starting amount owed:** Rs. 10,000
**Interest rate used in activity:** 10% for one year
**Interest amount:** Not shown yet

### Learner Question

What will Hamza owe after Rs. 1,000 of interest is charged?

### Options

1. Rs. 9,000
2. Rs. 10,000
3. **Rs. 11,000**
4. Rs. 20,000

### After State

**Original amount borrowed:** Rs. 10,000
**Interest charged:** Rs. 1,000
**Final amount owed:** Rs. 11,000
**Effect:** Hamza must repay Rs. 1,000 more than he borrowed.

### Explanation

Hamza pays interest in this activity. The added amount increases his repayment cost.

---

## Values Being Compared

| Comparison     |     Before |          Interest |            After |
| -------------- | ---------: | ----------------: | ---------------: |
| Sara’s savings | Rs. 10,000 |   Rs. 1,000 added | Rs. 11,000 owned |
| Hamza’s loan   | Rs. 10,000 | Rs. 1,000 charged |  Rs. 11,000 owed |

## Final Learner Question

Both balances became Rs. 11,000. What is the important difference?

### Options

1. **Sara owns Rs. 11,000; Hamza owes Rs. 11,000**
2. Both people earned Rs. 1,000
3. Both people received free money
4. Hamza no longer needs to repay anything

**Correct Answer:**
Sara owns Rs. 11,000; Hamza owes Rs. 11,000.

## Important Hidden Difference

The final numbers look the same, but they mean opposite things:

* Sara has **more money that belongs to her**.
* Hamza has **more money that he must repay**.

## Correct Completion State

The learner identifies:

* **Sara’s final savings balance:** Rs. 11,000
* **Sara’s interest effect:** Helps the saver in this activity
* **Hamza’s final amount owed:** Rs. 11,000
* **Hamza’s interest effect:** Costs the borrower
* **Hidden difference:** One balance is owned; the other is owed

## Validation Method

Check each after-value and the final hidden-difference answer. Complete the interaction when all three answers are correct.

## Success Feedback

Excellent comparison. The same Rs. 1,000 increase helps Sara but increases Hamza’s repayment.

## Incorrect Feedback

Look at whether the balance is money the person owns or money the person owes.

## Incomplete Feedback

Complete both comparisons and identify the hidden difference before finishing.

## Retry Behaviour

Keep correct answers marked. Return only the incorrect comparison, show the calculation, and allow unlimited retries.

## Accessibility Alternative

Present each comparison as a linear text card with radio-button answers. Read the before amount, interest amount, and after options aloud.

**Screen-reader instruction:**
Listen to the starting amount and interest. Choose the final balance, then decide whether the person owns or owes it.

**Primary Button Label:** Check My Comparison

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** Interest Changes a Balance

**Explanation:**
You saw both balances rise from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 11,000. The added Rs. 1,000 is **interest**. Interest is an amount added to money over time. A saver may earn it, so the savings balance grows. A borrower usually pays it, so the amount owed grows. Interest is not always free money: its effect depends on whether you are saving or borrowing. Real rates, time periods, fees, and calculation methods vary.

**Suggested Visual:**
One Rs. 1,000 arrow moves into Sara’s saving jar. Another Rs. 1,000 arrow is added to Hamza’s repayment note.

**Explain It Simply:**
Interest can grow savings or increase what a borrower must repay.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
A savings balance rises from Rs. 2,000 to Rs. 2,100 without another deposit. What most likely caused the extra Rs. 100?

### Options

1. **Interest added to the savings**
2. Money borrowed from the bank
3. A withdrawal
4. The original Rs. 2,000 disappearing

**Correct Answer:**
Interest added to the savings

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The saver did not add more money. In this example, the extra Rs. 100 was interest added over time.

**Incorrect Feedback — Money borrowed from the bank:**
Borrowed money creates an amount to repay. It is different from interest earned on savings.

**Incorrect Feedback — A withdrawal:**
A withdrawal removes money and would normally make the balance lower.

**Incorrect Feedback — The original Rs. 2,000 disappearing:**
The starting money remains in the account. The balance rose because an additional amount was added.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Savers and borrowers experience interest in the same way.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
A family borrows Rs. 5,000 and is charged Rs. 500 interest. How much must they repay in this simple example?

### Options

1. Rs. 4,500
2. Rs. 5,000
3. **Rs. 5,500**
4. Rs. 10,000

**Correct Answer:**
Rs. 5,500

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The borrower repays the original Rs. 5,000 plus Rs. 500 of interest.

**Incorrect Feedback — Rs. 4,500:**
Interest is added to the borrowed amount in this example; it is not subtracted.

**Incorrect Feedback — Rs. 5,000:**
Rs. 5,000 is only the original amount borrowed. The Rs. 500 interest must also be repaid.

**Incorrect Feedback — Rs. 10,000:**
The amount does not automatically double. Add the stated Rs. 500 interest to Rs. 5,000.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A borrower only needs to repay the original amount borrowed.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
Amina saves Rs. 3,000 and earns Rs. 300 interest. Bilal borrows Rs. 3,000 and is charged Rs. 300 interest. Which statement is correct?

### Options

1. **Amina owns Rs. 3,300; Bilal owes Rs. 3,300**
2. Both people earned Rs. 300
3. Bilal only owes Rs. 3,000
4. Interest was free money for both

**Correct Answer:**
Amina owns Rs. 3,300; Bilal owes Rs. 3,300

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Amina’s interest increases money she owns. Bilal’s interest increases money he must repay.

**Incorrect Feedback — Both people earned Rs. 300:**
Amina earned interest, but Bilal was charged interest.

**Incorrect Feedback — Bilal only owes Rs. 3,000:**
Bilal owes the original Rs. 3,000 plus the Rs. 300 interest charge.

**Incorrect Feedback — Interest was free money for both:**
The interest helps Amina’s savings but is an extra cost for Bilal.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Interest is always free money.
