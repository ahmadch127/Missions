# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J2-M11
**Journey:** Make Money Grow
**Mission Title:** Compound Growth
**Core Concept:** Compound growth happens when money earns returns and future returns are calculated on both the original money and its previous growth.
**Target Age Group:** Kids 8–12
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how earning returns on previous growth can make money grow faster over a long period.

**Primary Misconception:**
Compound growth creates huge results immediately.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Compound growth increases money by the same fixed amount every year.
* Time does not make a significant difference to how much money can grow.

**Key Takeaway:**
Compound growth becomes powerful with time.

## Educational Assumptions

* The activity begins with one £1,000 contribution.
* No additional money is added or withdrawn.
* A fixed 5% classroom return is added once per year.
* Each year’s return stays in the balance.
* Each displayed yearly return is rounded to the nearest penny before it is added.
* No fees, taxes, or other charges are included.
* Real returns may be lower, higher, or negative and can change over time.
* This activity is an educational example, not a prediction or guaranteed result.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** When Growth Earns Growth

**Explanation:**
You moved through 20 years and saw the yearly addition rise from £50 to £126.35. This is **compound growth**: future returns are calculated on the original money and earlier growth left in the balance. It often looks slow at first because the balance is still small. Over longer periods, the larger balance can produce larger additions. The 5% rate is only a classroom example. Real returns can change, fall, or become negative, so growth is never guaranteed.

**Suggested Visual:**
One mango tree grows into a small orchard while the balance timeline curves upward gradually rather than jumping immediately.

**Explain It Simply:**
Growth can earn more growth when it stays in the balance.

**Primary Button Label:** See Another Example

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
A practice balance grows from £100 to £110. The next 10% return is calculated on £110. What does that show?

### Options

1. **Previous growth can earn more growth**
2. The original money disappeared
3. The return must stay at £10

**Correct Answer:**
Previous growth can earn more growth

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The balance used for the next calculation includes both the original £100 and the earlier £10 growth.

**Incorrect Feedback — The original money disappeared:**
The original £100 remains in the balance together with the £10 of growth.

**Incorrect Feedback — The return must stay at £10:**
Ten per cent of £110 is £11, so the next addition can be larger.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Compound growth increases money by the same fixed amount every year.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
A £1,000 balance earns a 5% classroom return for one year. What should you expect?

### Options

1. **A small rise to £1,050**
2. An instant rise to £5,000
3. A guaranteed fortune
4. No change for twenty years

**Correct Answer:**
A small rise to £1,050

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Five per cent of £1,000 is £50, so the classroom balance becomes £1,050 after one year.

**Incorrect Feedback — An instant rise to £5,000:**
A 5% return adds £50 to £1,000, not £4,000.

**Incorrect Feedback — A guaranteed fortune:**
The 5% rate is only a classroom assumption. Real returns are uncertain and never guarantee wealth.

**Incorrect Feedback — No change for twenty years:**
In this activity, the first return is added after Year 1.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Compound growth creates huge results immediately.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
Two practice balances start at £500 with the same yearly rate. One stays for 2 years and one for 10 years. Which usually gains more in this activity?

### Options

1. **The 10-year balance**
2. The 2-year balance
3. They must always finish equal
4. Time makes no difference

**Correct Answer:**
The 10-year balance

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Under the same positive classroom rate, the 10-year balance has more periods for growth to build on earlier growth.

**Incorrect Feedback — The 2-year balance:**
The shorter timeline provides fewer chances for growth to build on previous growth.

**Incorrect Feedback — They must always finish equal:**
With the same classroom rate and no withdrawals, the longer timeline produces more growth in this activity.

**Incorrect Feedback — Time makes no difference:**
Time provides repeated chances for returns to be added to the growing balance.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Time does not make a significant difference to how much money can grow.
