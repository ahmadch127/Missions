## Concepts Being Assessed

- Inflation and purchasing power
- Budget adjustment
- Saving and interest
- Basic investment growth and uncertainty
- Assets and liabilities
- Responsible debt decisions

## Learning Objective

Apply inflation, saving, budgeting, interest, and asset-versus-liability concepts to protect purchasing power as prices rise.

## Primary Combined Misconception

Keeping the same amount of money guarantees that a person will be able to afford the same things in the future.

## Secondary Misconceptions

- Cash always protects money from financial risk.
- Any item that may help someone earn money is automatically a good asset.
- A higher savings balance always means purchasing power has increased.

## Key Takeaway

**Money must grow or plans must adjust when prices rise faster than savings.**

# 2. Boss Mission Overview

Manage Rs. 100,000 across one year as a laptop rises from Rs. 100,000 to Rs. 115,000. Choose where to keep money, rebuild a monthly budget after prices rise, and judge asset and debt choices. Earlier decisions change access, goal progress, debt, and the final dashboard. The score rewards realistic decisions rather than luck.

# Stage 1 — Purchasing Power

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J2-BM2-ST1  
**Timeline:** Start of Year vs One Year Later  
**Interaction:** Before Vs After  
**Concepts:** inflation, purchasing_power

## Situation

One year ago, Rs. 100,000 bought one laptop. Today, savings are still Rs. 100,000, but the laptop costs Rs. 115,000.

## Learner Objective

Identify whether purchasing power increased, decreased, or stayed the same.

## Before vs After

| Value | Before | One Year Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Savings balance | Rs. 100,000 | Rs. 100,000 |
| Laptop price | Rs. 100,000 | Rs. 115,000 |
| Laptops affordable | 1.00 | 0.87 |

**Question:** Has the purchasing power of Rs. 100,000 increased, decreased, or remained the same?

**Important Hidden Difference:** The balance is unchanged, but the goal price increased.

**Explanation:** Rs. 100,000 now covers about 87% of the laptop. Purchasing power decreased.

## Available Choices or Completion States

### Increased

**Classification:** Incorrect

**Immediate Feedback:** The laptop became more expensive while savings did not rise.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The price gap is underestimated.

**Later Consequence:** The plan may not adjust for inflation.

### Decreased

**Classification:** Recommended

**Immediate Feedback:** Correct. The same money no longer buys the same laptop.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The Rs. 15,000 gap becomes visible.

**Later Consequence:** Storage and saving decisions can address the real goal.

### Stayed the same

**Classification:** Incorrect

**Immediate Feedback:** The number stayed the same, but what it can buy changed.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Balance is confused with purchasing power.

**Later Consequence:** The learner may fail to adapt.

## Recommended Decision Logic

**Generally Strongest:** Compare what money can buy, not only the account number.


**Trade-Off:** A stable number may still lose purchasing power.

# Stage 2 — Choose Where to Keep Money

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J2-BM2-ST2  
**Timeline:** Start of the Year  
**Interaction:** Decision Tree  
**Concepts:** bank_accounts, interest, compound_growth, basic_investment_risk, purchasing_power

## Situation

Rewind to the start of the year. The laptop may be needed within twelve months. Choose how to store Rs. 100,000 while balancing access, growth, and uncertainty.

## Learner Objective

Choose a storage approach that fits the goal date and ability to accept losses.

## Educational Assumptions

- All rates and outcomes are fictional classroom assumptions.
- The savings-account path uses 5% illustrative growth for one year.
- The mixed path places Rs. 60,000 in savings and Rs. 40,000 in a basic investment.
- The displayed investment change is +8%, but a real investment could rise or fall.
- Fees and taxes are excluded.
- Decision quality is scored separately from the displayed result.
- No real product is recommended.

## Available Choices or Completion States

### Keep everything as cash

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Feedback:** The amount stays accessible but earns no growth in this simulation.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Accessible cash remains Rs. 100,000.

**Later Consequence:** The laptop gap remains Rs. 15,000 before new contributions.

### Use a savings account

**Classification:** Recommended

**Immediate Feedback:** The money remains accessible and earns Rs. 5,000 of illustrative growth.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The goal balance becomes Rs. 105,000.

**Later Consequence:** The gap becomes Rs. 10,000 before new contributions.

### Spend everything immediately

**Classification:** Risky

**Immediate Feedback:** The money is no longer available for the laptop or unexpected needs.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Savings fall to zero.

**Later Consequence:** The full Rs. 115,000 target must be rebuilt or borrowed.

### Split Rs. 60,000 into savings and Rs. 40,000 into a basic investment

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Feedback:** Some money stays accessible while another part accepts investment uncertainty.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The displayed path ends with Rs. 63,000 in savings and Rs. 43,200 invested.

**Later Consequence:** The total reaches Rs. 106,200, but the invested part may not suit a near-term goal.

## Recommended Decision Logic

**Generally Strongest:** For a goal likely needed within twelve months, a savings account is generally strong because access and lower uncertainty matter.

**Other Reasonable Paths:**

- Cash may suit very short-term access while accepting weaker growth.
- A mixed approach may suit money not needed soon when losses are acceptable.

**Trade-Off:** More growth potential usually means more uncertainty or less immediate access.

## Updated State Screen

**Title:** Storage Strategy Applied

- **Available Cash:** 100000 → selected_path.available_cash. The approach changes access.
- **Illustrative Savings Growth:** 0 → selected_path.illustrative_savings_growth. The savings paths earn classroom growth.
- **Investment Balance:** 0 → selected_path.investment_balance. The mixed path includes an uncertain result.

**Learning Message:** Storage should fit the goal date, access needs, and risk tolerance.

**Next-Stage Teaser:** Rising monthly costs now force the budget to change.

## Retry Behaviour

Allow review of every trade-off before confirmation.

## Accessibility Alternative

Use four labelled choice buttons. Choose a storage approach and hear the access, growth, and risk effects.

# Stage 4 — Asset or Cost?

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J2-BM2-ST4  
**Timeline:** End of the Year  
**Interaction:** Sort Categories Decision Tree  
**Concepts:** assets_and_liabilities, responsible_debt, affordability, purchasing_power, budget_stability

## Situation

Your storage choice and monthly saving plan have created a projected goal fund. Classify four financial choices, then decide how to handle the Rs. 115,000 laptop goal.

## Learner Objective

Judge value by actual use and affordability, then choose a goal action that fits the current state.

## Sort Categories

- **Can Generate Value**
- **Mainly Costs Money**
- **Depends on Use and Affordability**

## Sortable Items

| Item | Recommended Category | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laptop used for paid work | Can Generate Value | It may support income, but price, maintenance, and actual use still matter. |
| Luxury phone purchased with debt | Mainly Costs Money | It creates repayments and may lose value without producing income. |
| Course that improves work skills | Depends on Use and Affordability | Quality, suitability, and affordability must be checked; results are not guaranteed. |
| Unused subscription | Mainly Costs Money | It creates repeated cost without meaningful use. |

**Final Decision Question:** What should you do about the Rs. 115,000 laptop goal?

## Available Choices or Completion States

### Buy the laptop when the goal fund covers it and the work use is clear

**Classification:** Recommended

**Immediate Feedback:** The purchase is linked to a funded goal and a specific work purpose.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Rs. 115,000 is deducted from the goal fund.

**Later Consequence:** The remaining balance stays available and no new debt is created.

### Cancel the unused subscription and continue saving

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Feedback:** A repeated unnecessary cost is removed and future saving improves.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Avoidable expenses fall by Rs. 1,500 monthly.

**Later Consequence:** The laptop gap closes without new debt, but the purchase happens later.

### Borrow only the remaining laptop gap with a clear repayment plan

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Feedback:** The debt supports a work tool, but repayments must fit the budget.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Borrow up to Rs. 15,000 using six equal, interest-free classroom payments.

**Later Consequence:** Budget stability falls if the repayment does not fit beside essentials and saving.

### Buy a luxury phone with Rs. 100,000 of debt

**Classification:** Risky

**Immediate Feedback:** The purchase creates a large obligation without supporting the laptop goal.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Debt increases by Rs. 100,000.

**Later Consequence:** Budget stability and purchasing-power protection weaken.

### Spend Rs. 30,000 on a course without checking quality or affordability

**Classification:** Risky

**Immediate Feedback:** A course may create value, but this decision lacks evidence and affordability checks.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The goal fund falls by Rs. 30,000.

**Later Consequence:** The laptop may be delayed and the course benefit remains uncertain.

## Recommended Decision Logic

**Generally Strongest:** Buy the laptop when it is funded, genuinely supports paid work, and does not destabilise the budget.

**Other Reasonable Paths:**

- Continue saving after cancelling an unnecessary expense when the goal is underfunded.
- Borrowing only a small gap may be reasonable when the laptop supports credible income and repayments fit.

**Trade-Off:** Buying sooner may support work, while waiting or borrowing changes timing, access, and monthly pressure.

**Learning Message:** An asset depends on use, affordability, and the pressure it creates.

**Next-Stage Teaser:** Review how well the year protected purchasing power.

**Question:** Your savings balance is higher than last year. What must be checked before saying purchasing power improved?

**Correct Explanation:** A higher balance protects purchasing power only when it keeps pace with relevant prices.

**Concepts Tested:** inflation, interest, purchasing_power

**Misconception Corrected:** A higher savings balance always means purchasing power increased.
