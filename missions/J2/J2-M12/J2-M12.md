# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J2-M12
**Journey:** Make Money Grow
**Mission Title:** Good Debt and Harmful Debt
**Core Concept:** Debt should be evaluated by its purpose, total cost, repayment terms, and whether the borrower can afford the repayments.
**Target Age Group:** Kids 8–12
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand that debt can be more or less useful depending on why the money is borrowed, how much it costs, and whether it can be repaid comfortably.

**Primary Misconception:**
All debt is equally harmful.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Borrowing for education or a business is always a good decision.
* A small monthly repayment means a loan is automatically affordable.

**Key Takeaway:**
Debt is not automatically good or bad; purpose, cost, terms, and affordability matter.

# 4. Pre-Interaction Question

**Screen Title:** What Should You Check?

**Question:**
What information matters most before deciding whether debt may be useful or risky?

## Option 1

**Answer:** Purpose, total cost, repayments, and affordability
**Recommended Answer:** Yes

**Response:**
Good thinking. A useful purpose is only part of the decision. The full cost and effect on essential spending also matter.

## Option 2

**Answer:** Whether the monthly repayment sounds small

**Response:**
A small payment may continue for many months. The total amount repaid could still be high.

## Option 3

**Answer:** Whether the purchase looks important

**Response:**
An important-looking purchase can still have expensive or unaffordable loan terms.

## Option 4

**Answer:** Whether someone else also borrowed

**Response:**
Another person’s decision does not show whether the loan fits this borrower’s income and budget.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Sort Categories

**Interaction Title:** Sort the Borrowing Decisions

**Instruction:**
Place each borrowing decision under Potentially Useful, Risky, or Depends on Situation. Use all the details, not only the purchase.

## Educational Assumptions

* The examples involve adults or families making borrowing decisions.
* A trusted adult reviews the loan details.
* The stated repayments and total costs are simplified classroom examples.
* A useful purchase does not guarantee future income or success.
* Real loans may include different interest rates, fees, penalties, and repayment conditions.
* The activity does not recommend taking any loan.

## Categories

### Potentially Useful Debt

Borrowing that may create lasting value, has manageable terms, and leaves enough money for essential needs.

### Risky Debt

Borrowing that is difficult to afford, mainly pays for an optional purchase, or disrupts essential spending.

### Depends on Situation

Borrowing that could help or harm depending on the loan cost, repayment plan, income, timing, and available alternatives.

* Repayment: £55 per month for 12 months
* The family can cover the repayment without reducing food, transport, or emergency savings

## Context-Dependent Items

The following decisions depend strongly on the situation:

* Borrowing for everyday expenses
* Borrowing for education with high fees or unaffordable repayments
* Borrowing for a business or work tool without clear demand or a manageable repayment plan

## Explanation of Ambiguity

The same purchase can belong in different categories when the loan terms or family situation change.

For example:

* An affordable education loan may be potentially useful.
* An education loan with very high fees or repayments that reduce grocery money may be risky.
* A work tool may help create income, but it is not automatically a good loan if no customers exist or repayments are unaffordable.

## Placement Rules

* Every item must be placed in one category.
* Use the purpose, total repayment, repayment amount, and effect on essential needs.
* Each item has one recommended category based on the details provided.
* The category applies only to this scenario, not to every similar loan.

## Validation Method

Check all five items after submission. Keep correct items in place and return incorrect items with a short explanation.

## Success Feedback

Excellent sorting. You checked more than the reason for borrowing—you also considered cost, repayment terms, and essential spending.

## Incorrect Feedback

Look beyond the purchase. Does the total repayment fit the budget without reducing money for essential needs?

## Incomplete Feedback

Sort all five borrowing decisions before checking your answer.

## Retry Behaviour

Keep correctly sorted items in place. Return only incorrect items to the centre, provide one short hint, and allow unlimited retries.

## Accessibility Alternative

Present each borrowing decision as a card with three buttons: Potentially Useful, Risky, and Depends on Situation.

**Screen-reader instruction:**
Listen to the loan purpose, total cost, monthly repayment, and effect on essential spending. Then choose the most suitable category.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which borrowing decision appears potentially useful in the situation described?

### Options

1. **A work tool with affordable repayments and confirmed orders**
2. A luxury watch that reduces grocery money
3. A new phone when the current phone works
4. A loan with unknown fees and no repayment plan

**Correct Answer:**
A work tool with affordable repayments and confirmed orders

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The tool may create lasting value, there is a clear use for it, and the repayments leave essential spending protected.

**Incorrect Feedback — A luxury watch that reduces grocery money:**
The purchase is optional, and the repayment would make an essential expense harder to cover.

**Incorrect Feedback — A new phone when the current phone works:**
An optional upgrade may not create enough lasting value to justify borrowing costs.

**Incorrect Feedback — A loan with unknown fees and no repayment plan:**
Without the total cost and repayment plan, it is difficult to judge whether the borrowing is affordable.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A debt’s purpose is the only information needed to judge it.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Which statement about debt is most accurate?

### Options

1. **Debt can be useful or harmful depending on its terms and affordability**
2. Every debt is equally harmful
3. Education debt is always good
4. A loan is affordable whenever the monthly payment is small

**Correct Answer:**
Debt can be useful or harmful depending on its terms and affordability

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The purpose matters, but the total cost, repayment period, income, and money remaining for essentials also affect the result.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every debt is equally harmful:**
Some borrowing may create lasting value and remain affordable, while other borrowing can cause financial pressure.

**Incorrect Feedback — Education debt is always good:**
Education may be useful, but high fees or unaffordable repayments can still make the loan harmful.

**Incorrect Feedback — A loan is affordable whenever the monthly payment is small:**
A small payment may continue for a long time and create a high total repayment.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
All debt is equally harmful.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A tablet costs £500, but a loan asks for £15 monthly for 60 months. What should the borrower check first?

### Options

1. **The £900 total repayment and whether it fits the budget**
2. Only whether £15 sounds small
3. Whether the tablet looks exciting
4. Whether the first payment is affordable

**Correct Answer:**
The £900 total repayment and whether it fits the budget

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Sixty payments of £15 total £900. The borrower must consider the full cost and whether all repayments leave enough for essential needs.

**Incorrect Feedback — Only whether £15 sounds small:**
The monthly amount is only one part of the loan. Sixty payments create a much larger total cost.

**Incorrect Feedback — Whether the tablet looks exciting:**
Excitement does not show whether the loan is affordable or good value.

**Incorrect Feedback — Whether the first payment is affordable:**
The borrower must be able to manage the full repayment period, not only the first month.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A small monthly repayment means a loan is automatically affordable.
