# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J5-M30
**Journey:** Master Money Decisions
**Mission Title:** Build Your Financial Life
**Core Concept:** Financial strength comes from combining budgeting, saving, emergency planning, responsible spending, scam awareness, debt management, and long-term investing.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 8 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-PK
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Apply multiple money-management and investing concepts to make balanced financial decisions across one simulated year.

**Primary Misconception:**
Wealth usually comes from one lucky or highly profitable investment.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* One financial mistake permanently ruins a person’s financial future.
* Investing should always come before budgeting and emergency savings.

**Key Takeaway:**
Financial strength usually comes from many consistent decisions, not one perfect choice.

## Budget Feedback

### Essentials Are Not Covered

**Feedback:**
The budget may not be sustainable because a required expense is underfunded.

### No Money Is Saved

**Feedback:**
An unexpected expense may create debt or force another goal to be abandoned.

### All Entertainment Is Removed

**Feedback:**
The plan may be mathematically valid but difficult to maintain.

### Needs, Savings, and Wants Are Included

**Feedback:**
The plan is more balanced and gives several financial priorities a place.

## Budget Validation

A valid budget must:

1. Cover each required minimum.
2. Total exactly Rs. 100,000.
3. Avoid negative balances.
4. Assign something to emergency savings, long-term savings, or planned investing.

# Stage 3 — Manage a Surprise Expense

## Timing

**Month 4**

## Event

An essential appliance breaks. The repair costs **Rs. 35,000**.

## Choice 1 — Use Emergency Savings

**Consequence:**
The emergency fund pays as much of the repair as possible. Any small remaining amount comes from general savings.

**Balance Rule:**
Deduct from the emergency fund first, then general savings.

**Confidence points:** 15 of 15

## Choice 2 — Borrow the Full Amount

**Consequence:**
The repair is completed, but debt increases by Rs. 35,000 and future income must cover repayment.

**Confidence points:** 5 of 15

## Choice 3 — Skip an Essential Payment

**Consequence:**
The repair is completed, but a Rs. 35,000 unpaid essential obligation is created.

**Confidence points:** 0 of 15

## Choice 4 — Use Savings and Reduce Optional Spending Temporarily

**Consequence:**
Some general savings cover the repair, and next month’s wants budget is temporarily reduced.

**Confidence points:** 12 of 15

## Outcome Feedback

Emergency savings protect the normal budget. Borrowing can solve the immediate problem but creates a future obligation. Skipping essential expenses may create a larger problem.

# Stage 4 — Avoid a Financial Scam

## Timing

**Month 6**

## Message

“Invest Rs. 25,000 today and receive Rs. 50,000 next week. Profit is guaranteed. Do not discuss this opportunity with your bank.”

## Choice 1 — Send the Money Immediately

**Consequence:**
In this fictional scenario, the offer is fraudulent and Rs. 25,000 is lost from available savings.

**Confidence points:** 0 of 15

## Choice 2 — Ask a Friend Whether It Looks Genuine

**Consequence:**
A second opinion may help, but a friend may also rely on the same false message.

**Confidence points:** 8 of 15

## Choice 3 — Research Through Independent Sources

**Recommended choice:** Yes

**Consequence:**
You check the company, contact details, authorisation, payment destination, risks, and complaints before sending money.

**Confidence points:** 15 of 15

## Choice 4 — Trust the Professional Website

**Consequence:**
The design looks convincing, but logos, certificates, testimonials, and websites can be copied.

**Confidence points:** 2 of 15

## Scam Warning Signs

* Guaranteed high return
* Very short deadline
* Secrecy request
* Pressure to bypass the bank
* Immediate payment demand

## Stage Lesson

Independent verification is more reliable than appearance, urgency, or social recommendations.

**Recommended choice:** Yes

**Example goal:**
Save Rs. 180,000 over 18 months by contributing Rs. 10,000 monthly.

**Consequence:**

**Confidence points:** 4 of 10

## Stage Lesson

Wants are not automatically bad. They should be balanced with affordability, emergency readiness, debt, and long-term priorities.

# Financial State Rules

## Monthly Transfers

After the budget is approved:

* Emergency allocation moves to the emergency fund each month.
* Long-term savings move to general cash savings each month.
* Planned-investing money remains in general savings through Month 7.
* From Month 8, it moves into investments only if the learner activates a regular investment plan.
* Entertainment and essential-expense allocations are treated as spent during the month.

## Appliance Repair

Deduct the selected funding source according to the learner’s choice.

## Scam Loss

If the learner sends the money, deduct Rs. 25,000 from general savings.

## Market Drop

At Month 10:

**Updated investment value = current investment value × 0.85**

## Debt

Debt increases when the learner:

* Borrows for the repair
* Creates an unpaid essential obligation
* Borrows to invest
* Uses debt for the phone

No invented interest rate is applied.

# Trade-Off Summary

| Decision                  | Possible Benefit             | Main Risk                                   |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Save more for emergencies | Greater protection           | Less money for current wants or other goals |
| Borrow for a surprise     | Solves the immediate problem | Creates future repayment obligations        |
| Invest more money         | Greater growth potential     | Greater exposure to loss                    |
| Keep more cash            | Greater short-term access    | Lower growth potential and inflation risk   |
| Delay a want              | Protects current stability   | The purchase happens later                  |
| Spend on enjoyment        | Supports a sustainable life  | May delay other goals if unaffordable       |

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which situation shows the strongest overall financial foundation?

### Options

1. One investment rises quickly, but bills and emergencies depend on debt
2. All money remains in cash with no goals or plan
3. **Essentials are covered, savings exist, debt is controlled, and investing fits the plan**
4. Every want is removed permanently

**Correct Answer:**
Essentials are covered, savings exist, debt is controlled, and investing fits the plan

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Financial strength comes from several balanced systems rather than one isolated result.

**Incorrect Feedback — One investment rises quickly:**
A large gain does not replace essential expenses, emergency preparation, or debt control.

**Incorrect Feedback — All money remains in cash:**
Cash may be useful, but goals, inflation, and long-term decisions still need a plan.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every want is removed:**
A sustainable plan may include affordable enjoyment as well as future goals.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
The highest investment balance is the only measure of financial strength.
