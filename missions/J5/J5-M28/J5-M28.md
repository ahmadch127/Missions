**Mission ID:** J5-M28
**Journey:** Master Money Decisions
**Mission Title:** Spot Financial Scams
**Core Concept:** Financial scams often use unrealistic promises, urgency, secrecy, and social pressure to make people send money before verifying an opportunity.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 5 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-PK
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Identify common warning signs that may indicate a financial offer or investment opportunity is a scam.

**Primary Misconception:**
Professional-looking messages are automatically trustworthy.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A guaranteed return means an opportunity is safer.
* An offer must be genuine when a friend recommends it or shares it in a trusted group.

**Key Takeaway:**
Guaranteed high returns and pressure to act quickly are major warning signs.

**Interaction Type:** Sort Categories

**Interaction Title:** Sort the Financial Messages

**Instruction:**

## Educational Assumptions

* Every company, offer, testimonial, and message in this activity is fictional.
* A warning sign increases concern but may not independently prove fraud.
* Several warning signs together should increase concern further.
* A statement with too little information is not automatically safe or fraudulent.
* Normal financial wording does not prove that the person or company using it is genuine.
* Logos, websites, certificates, reviews, and screenshots can be copied or fabricated.
* Independent verification remains necessary before sending money or personal information.
* The activity does not recommend any real financial company or investment.

---

## Categories

### Category 1 — Scam Warning Sign

Statements containing unrealistic promises, urgency, secrecy, suspicious payment requests, withdrawal demands, or recruitment pressure.

### Category 2 — Not Enough Information

Statements that may be true but do not provide enough evidence to judge whether the opportunity is safe or fraudulent.

### Category 3 — Normal Financial Information

Statements that openly acknowledge risk or encourage research, verification, and review of fees and conditions.

# Context-Dependent Items

The following statements are not automatically safe or fraudulent:

* “This investment may produce returns over time.”
* “Contact us to learn more.”
* “The company says it invests in property.”
* “Past customers have reported positive results.”

They belong in **Not Enough Information** because their truth cannot be established from the wording alone.

## Explanation of Ambiguity

A genuine provider and a scammer may both use neutral or professional language. Classification should depend on evidence, independent verification, payment details, risks, fees, authorisation, and behaviour—not presentation alone.

# Safety Check Before Sending Money

The learner should:

1. Pause and avoid acting under pressure.
2. Research the company independently.
3. Check official registration or authorisation records.
4. Verify contact details using trusted sources.
5. Read the risks, fees, and withdrawal conditions.
6. Check where the money is being sent.
7. Avoid sharing passwords, PINs, OTPs, or private banking details.
8. Ask a trusted person or qualified adviser for a second opinion.
9. Search for complaints, impersonation warnings, or conflicting company details.
10. Keep records of messages, account details, and payment requests.

---

# Important Clarification

One warning sign does not always prove that an offer is fraudulent.

However:

* Several warning signs together should increase concern.
* A lack of obvious warning signs does not guarantee safety.
* Professional design does not prove legitimacy.
* Independent verification is still required.
* Research reduces uncertainty but cannot guarantee an investment result.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which message contains the strongest scam warning sign?

### Options

1. “Investment values may rise or fall.”
2. “Read the withdrawal terms carefully.”
3. **“Guaranteed 50% profit—pay within five minutes.”**
4. “Research the company independently.”

**Correct Answer:**
“Guaranteed 50% profit—pay within five minutes.”

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The message combines an unrealistic guarantee with pressure to act before checking the offer.

**Incorrect Feedback — “Investment values may rise or fall.”:**
This is a normal acknowledgement of investment risk.

**Incorrect Feedback — “Read the withdrawal terms carefully.”:**
This encourages the person to review important conditions.

**Incorrect Feedback — “Research the company independently.”:**
Independent research reduces reliance on the seller’s claims.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
All professional financial statements carry the same level of risk.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
A message has a professional website, company logo, testimonials, and certificate images. What does this prove?

### Options

1. The company is authorised
2. The investment is safe
3. The testimonials are genuine
4. **Nothing until the details are independently verified**

**Correct Answer:**
Nothing until the details are independently verified

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Websites, logos, certificates, and testimonials can be copied, altered, or fabricated.

**Incorrect Feedback — The company is authorised:**
Authorisation must be checked using official, independently located records.

**Incorrect Feedback — The investment is safe:**
Professional presentation does not remove investment or fraud risk.

**Incorrect Feedback — The testimonials are genuine:**
Testimonials may be selective, paid, copied, or invented.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Professional-looking messages are automatically trustworthy.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
