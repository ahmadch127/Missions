# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J5-M29
**Journey:** Master Money Decisions
**Mission Title:** Set Financial Goals
**Core Concept:** A financial goal turns a general wish into a measurable plan by defining an amount, deadline, monthly contribution, and priority.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 5 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-PK
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Learn how to turn a vague financial wish into a measurable goal with a target amount, deadline, priority, and contribution plan.

**Primary Misconception:**
Motivation alone is enough to reach a financial goal.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A goal does not need a specific amount or deadline.
* All financial goals should receive the same priority.

**Key Takeaway:**
A financial goal needs an amount, deadline, and contribution plan.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Budget Builder

**Interaction Title:** Turn Wishes Into Plans

**Instruction:**
Set an amount, deadline, monthly contribution, and priority for each goal. Keep the total within your Rs. 25,000 monthly savings budget.

## Educational Assumptions

* The learner has Rs. 25,000 available monthly after essential expenses.
* Rent, bills, food, debt payments, and other essentials are outside this activity and must not be reduced to complete the challenge.
* All goals and amounts are fictional examples.
* No interest or investment returns are included.
* Prices remain unchanged during the activity.
* Monthly contributions are made consistently.
* A goal is considered funded when monthly contribution multiplied by deadline equals or exceeds the goal amount.
* Actual costs, income, and priorities may change.
* Adjusting a goal is not failure.
* The activity does not provide personalised financial advice.

---

# Goal Status Rules

## On Track

Monthly contribution × deadline is equal to or greater than the goal amount.

## Underfunded

Monthly contribution × deadline is less than the goal amount.

## Over Budget

Total monthly contributions exceed Rs. 25,000.

## Undefined

The amount, deadline, contribution, or priority is missing.

## Paused

The learner has intentionally delayed the goal and must acknowledge that its original deadline will not be met.

---

# Suggested Minimum Monthly Contribution

**Formula:**

Goal amount ÷ deadline

Round up to the next Rs. 1,000 contribution step.

## Examples

### Laptop

Rs. 150,000 ÷ 15 months = Rs. 10,000 monthly

### Emergency Fund

Rs. 120,000 ÷ 12 months = Rs. 10,000 monthly

### Trip

Rs. 90,000 ÷ 18 months = Rs. 5,000 monthly

### Further Education

Rs. 240,000 ÷ 24 months = Rs. 10,000 monthly

# Priority Rules

* High-priority goals should be funded before lower-priority goals receive optional extra contributions.
* A high priority does not guarantee that the goal can be completed immediately.
* More than one goal may be high priority.
* Lower-priority goals are not irresponsible.
* A low-priority goal may receive a smaller contribution, longer deadline, or temporary pause.
* Priorities should reflect urgency and personal circumstances.
* The builder does not force one universal priority order.

## Classroom Recommendation

For this scenario:

* Emergency Fund: High
* Further Education: High
* Laptop: Medium
* Trip: Low

These are classroom examples, not personalised recommendations.

# Multiple Valid Plans

**Yes.**

A valid plan does not need to match the example exactly.

A plan passes when:

* Every active goal has an amount.
* Every active goal has a deadline.
* Every active goal has a priority.
* Every active goal has enough planned contributions to meet its amount.
* Total monthly contributions do not exceed Rs. 25,000.
* High-priority goals are not left underfunded while lower-priority goals receive optional extra money.
* Any paused goal clearly shows that its original deadline will not be met.

# Cause-and-Effect Feedback

## Choose a Goal Without an Amount

**Effect:**
Progress cannot be measured clearly.

## Choose a Goal Without a Deadline

**Effect:**
There is no time target, so saving may be delayed.

## Set a Contribution Below the Required Amount

**Effect:**
The goal will remain underfunded at the selected deadline.

## Set an Unrealistic Monthly Contribution

**Effect:**
The plan may exceed available savings or interfere with essential expenses.

## Give Every Goal Equal Priority

**Effect:**
Urgent goals may not receive enough money when the monthly budget is limited.

## Extend a Deadline

**Effect:**
The required monthly contribution becomes smaller, but the goal takes longer to reach.

## Reduce the Goal Amount

**Effect:**
The goal may become more affordable, but the final purchase or experience may need to change.

## Pause a Goal

**Effect:**
More money becomes available for other goals, but the paused goal will be delayed.

## Review Progress Regularly

**Effect:**
Amounts, deadlines, contributions, and priorities can be adjusted when circumstances change.

# Validation and Scoring Rules

## Required for Completion

1. All four goals must be reviewed.
2. Every active goal must have an amount above Rs. 0.
3. Every active goal must have a deadline between 3 and 60 months.
4. Every active goal must have a priority.
5. Monthly contribution × deadline must meet or exceed the amount.
6. Total monthly contributions must not exceed Rs. 25,000.
7. All high-priority goals must be on track.
8. The learner must answer both decision checks correctly.

## Scoring

### 3 Stars — Complete and Prioritised

* All active goals are on track.
* Monthly total is Rs. 25,000 or less.
* High-priority goals are funded.
* No essential-expense money is used.

### 2 Stars — Affordable but Needs Review

* The plan fits the budget.
* One lower-priority goal is underfunded or paused without a revised deadline.

### 1 Star — Measurable but Over Budget

* Goals have amounts and deadlines.
* Total contributions exceed Rs. 25,000.

The learner must revise a one-star plan before completing the mission.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which plan is the most measurable?

### Options

1. Save for a laptop soon
2. Try to spend less
3. **Save Rs. 120,000 in 12 months by contributing Rs. 10,000 monthly**
4. Stay motivated throughout the year

**Correct Answer:**
Save Rs. 120,000 in 12 months by contributing Rs. 10,000 monthly

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The plan defines the target amount, deadline, and monthly contribution.

**Incorrect Feedback — Save for a laptop soon:**
The amount and meaning of “soon” are unclear.

**Incorrect Feedback — Try to spend less:**
This may support a goal, but it does not define the target or contribution.

**Incorrect Feedback — Stay motivated throughout the year:**
Motivation is useful, but progress still needs measurable actions.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A financial goal does not require a specific amount or deadline.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Someone feels highly motivated to save Rs. 180,000 but has no deadline or monthly contribution. What is missing?

### Options

1. More motivation
2. A more expensive goal
3. A guaranteed return
4. **A measurable contribution plan**

**Correct Answer:**
A measurable contribution plan

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The person needs a deadline and monthly action to measure progress towards Rs. 180,000.

**Incorrect Feedback — More motivation:**
Motivation does not calculate how much must be saved each month.

**Incorrect Feedback — A more expensive goal:**
Increasing the amount would not make the plan more measurable.

**Incorrect Feedback — A guaranteed return:**
A saving goal does not require a guaranteed investment result.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Motivation alone is enough to reach a financial goal.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A goal requires Rs. 120,000 in 12 months, but only Rs. 8,000 is saved monthly. What should be reviewed?

### Options

1. Nothing—the goal is already on track
2. **The amount, deadline, or monthly contribution**
3. Only the goal’s name
4. Whether every goal has equal priority

**Correct Answer:**
The amount, deadline, or monthly contribution

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Rs. 8,000 for 12 months provides Rs. 96,000, leaving the goal Rs. 24,000 short.

**Incorrect Feedback — Nothing—the goal is already on track:**
The planned contributions do not reach the target amount.

**Incorrect Feedback — Only the goal’s name:**
Changing the name does not fix the funding gap.

**Incorrect Feedback — Whether every goal has equal priority:**
Priorities matter, but the immediate problem is that this goal is underfunded.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A goal remains realistic even when its contribution does not match its amount and deadline.
