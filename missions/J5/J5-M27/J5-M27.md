# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J5-M27
**Journey:** Master Money Decisions
**Mission Title:** FOMO
**Core Concept:** Fear of missing out can pressure people into rushed investment decisions without properly understanding the opportunity or its risks.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-PK
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Recognise when fear, social pressure, and excitement are influencing an investment decision.

**Primary Misconception:**
If many people are buying an investment, the opportunity must be safe.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A friend’s large profit means another person will achieve the same result.
* Acting immediately is necessary whenever an investment price is rising.

**Key Takeaway:**
Fear of missing out can make people act before understanding the risk.

## Educational Assumptions

* The cryptocurrency, friend, platform, prices, and outcomes are fictional.
* The activity does not recommend buying or avoiding cryptocurrency.
* A popular investment may rise, fall, or remain near the same price.
* Research can improve understanding but cannot remove risk.

# Branch A — Buy Immediately

## Immediate Action

You invest all Rs. 30,000 without checking:

* What the asset does
* Why the price is rising
* Trading or withdrawal fees
* How easily it can be sold
* How much could be lost
* Whether it fits your financial goals

## Follow-Up Node A1

**Question:**
What is the biggest weakness in this decision?

### Options

1. The investment uses technology
2. **The decision is based mainly on urgency and excitement**
3. The amount is Rs. 30,000
4. The friend used social media

**Correct Answer:**
The decision is based mainly on urgency and excitement

**Explanation:**
The problem is not that the price may rise or fall. The problem is committing money before understanding the opportunity and risk.

## Possible Outcome A2 — Price Rises

The price rises after your purchase, and the investment becomes worth Rs. 34,500.

### Decision Check

Does the profit prove that buying immediately was a good decision process?

#### Options

1. Yes, profit proves the process was safe
2. **No, a risky decision can sometimes produce a positive result**
3. Yes, rising prices remove future risk
4. No, because investment profits are impossible

**Correct Answer:**
No, a risky decision can sometimes produce a positive result

**Explanation:**
A positive outcome does not prove the rushed decision was informed or repeatable.

## Possible Outcome A3 — Price Falls

The price falls sharply, and the investment becomes worth Rs. 19,500.

### Consequence

You may feel pressure to:

* Sell immediately
* Invest more to recover the loss
* Hide the loss
* Blame the friend
* Continue without understanding the asset

### Branch Lesson

Buying immediately may produce a gain or loss. The decision remains FOMO-driven because understanding was replaced by urgency.

## Terminal Outcome A

**Outcome Title:** Emotion Led the Purchase

**Summary:**
You acted quickly, but the final price did not change the fact that the original decision lacked research and risk checks.

# Branch B — Research First

## Immediate Action

You pause before transferring money.

You review:

* What the investment actually is
* How its price is determined
* Major risks
* Fees and withdrawal rules
* Price history and volatility
* Whether the platform is genuine
* Whether the investment fits your goal
* Whether you can afford a large loss

## Follow-Up Node B1

**Question:**
What does research guarantee?

### Options

1. A profit
2. The lowest purchase price
3. Protection from every scam
4. **Better understanding, but not a guaranteed result**

**Correct Answer:**
Better understanding, but not a guaranteed result

**Explanation:**
Research can reveal risks and missing information. It cannot control future prices.

## Follow-Up Node B2 — Decision Check

Before investing, answer:

1. Do I understand what I am buying?
2. Am I acting because of evidence or excitement?
3. Can I afford to lose some or all of this money?
4. Does it fit my goals and risk tolerance?
5. Am I relying only on someone else’s success story?

## Follow-Up Choice B3

After researching, what may you reasonably decide?

### Option 1 — Do Not Invest

You decide the opportunity is too unclear or does not fit your situation.

### Option 2 — Continue Researching

You decide that important information is still missing.

### Option 3 — Consider a Smaller Amount

You decide that even after research, any amount should reflect the possibility of loss.

### Option 4 — Follow a Different Plan

You decide to use your savings for another goal or investment process.

## Branch Lesson

Research does not force one decision. It replaces pressure with information.

## Terminal Outcome B

**Outcome Title:** Evidence Led the Decision

**Summary:**
You may invest, wait, choose a smaller amount, or avoid the opportunity. The important change is that fear no longer makes the choice for you.

* Your risk level will be similar

**Correct Answer:**
Timing, price, risk, fees, and circumstances may be different

**Explanation:**
Two people can buy the same asset at different prices and experience very different outcomes.

2. Your friend must know the future price

Your outcome remains uncertain

**Explanation:**

## Immediate Action

You refuse the rushed cryptocurrency purchase.

You then decide:

“Every investment must be dangerous, so I will never research another one.”

## Follow-Up Node D1

**Question:**
What is the weakness in this response?

### Options

1. Avoiding one rushed decision is always wrong
2. Every investment is safe
3. **It replaces one emotional reaction with another**
4. Research guarantees investment profits

**Correct Answer:**
It replaces one emotional reaction with another

**Explanation:**
Rejecting pressure can be sensible. Assuming every investment is automatically unsuitable is a separate decision that also lacks research.

## Follow-Up Node D2

What would be a more balanced response?

### Options

1. Buy anything popular
2. Never consider any opportunity
3. **Evaluate future opportunities individually**
4. Copy a different friend

**Correct Answer:**
Evaluate future opportunities individually

**Explanation:**
An opportunity should be assessed using its risks, quality, fees, suitability, and evidence.

## Terminal Outcome D

**Outcome Title:** Pressure Avoided, But Thinking Stopped

**Summary:**
You avoided one rushed purchase, but a blanket rejection can prevent informed evaluation of future opportunities.

# Decision Check

Before investing because of a popular post, ask:

* Do I understand what I am buying?
* What could cause the price to fall?
* Am I reacting to evidence or excitement?
* Am I being rushed by a countdown or warning?
* Can I afford to lose the amount?
* Does this fit my goal and time horizon?
* What fees or withdrawal limits apply?
* Am I relying only on profits shared by other people?
* Have I seen evidence of losses as well as gains?
* Would I still consider this without the social pressure?

---

# FOMO Warning Signals

* “Everyone is buying.”
* “This is your last chance.”
* “The price can only go up.”
* “My profit proves it works.”
* “Do not waste time researching.”
* “Borrow money before the opportunity disappears.”
* “You will regret missing this.”
* A countdown that creates urgency
* Profit screenshots without risk information
* Pressure to hide the decision from others

**Clarification:**
A warning signal does not automatically prove an investment is fraudulent or unsuitable. It means the learner should slow down and verify the opportunity.

---

# Trade-Off Summary

| Response         | Possible Benefit                                     | Main Risk                                          |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Buy immediately  | May enter before another price rise                  | Acts without understanding the risk                |
| Research first   | Builds understanding and reveals missing information | The price may move while research continues        |
| Copy the friend  | Feels simple and socially supported                  | Another person’s result may not apply              |
| Avoid everything | Prevents this rushed purchase                        | May reject future opportunities without evaluation |

## Recommended Learning Path

**Research first**

This is not a recommendation to invest. It is the recommended decision process because it separates evidence from social pressure.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** When Excitement Replaces Evidence

**Explanation:**
You saw how urgency, popularity, and someone else’s profit can influence a decision. This pressure is called **fear of missing out**, or **FOMO**. It can make people act before understanding an investment’s risks, fees, price, or suitability. A popular investment is not automatically safe or unsafe. Research can improve the decision process, but it cannot guarantee profit. The goal is not to predict every outcome—it is to avoid letting social pressure make the decision.

**Suggested Visual:**
A flashing Buy Now button fades while a checklist of evidence, risks, goals, and fees becomes clearer.

**Explain It Simply:**
FOMO creates urgency before understanding.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which situation most clearly shows FOMO influencing a decision?

### Options

1. Reviewing fees before investing
2. Comparing an investment with a financial goal
3. **Buying immediately because everyone online seems excited**
4. Checking whether a platform is genuine

**Correct Answer:**
Buying immediately because everyone online seems excited

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The decision is being driven by popularity and urgency rather than understanding.

**Incorrect Feedback — Reviewing fees before investing:**
This is part of an evidence-based review.

**Incorrect Feedback — Comparing an investment with a financial goal:**
This checks whether the opportunity fits the investor’s plan.

**Incorrect Feedback — Checking whether a platform is genuine:**
Verification can help reveal risks before money is transferred.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
FOMO is the same as normal investment research.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Thousands of people are buying an investment. What does this prove?

### Options

1. The investment is safe
2. The price must continue rising
3. Everyone will make the same profit
4. **Only that the investment is currently popular**

**Correct Answer:**
Only that the investment is currently popular

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Popularity may affect demand and price, but it does not prove safety, value, or future profit.

**Incorrect Feedback — The investment is safe:**
Many buyers can still be wrong or accept risks that do not fit another person.

**Incorrect Feedback — The price must continue rising:**
Prices can reverse even after strong demand.

**Incorrect Feedback — Everyone will make the same profit:**
People may buy at different prices, sell at different times, and pay different fees.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
If many people are buying, the opportunity must be safe.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A friend shows a large profit and tells you to buy immediately. What is the best first response?

### Options

1. Copy the same amount
2. Borrow money before the price rises
3. Assume the result will be identical
4. **Pause and understand the opportunity and risks**

**Correct Answer:**
Pause and understand the opportunity and risks

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The friend’s result may involve a different entry price, risk level, holding period, and financial situation.

**Incorrect Feedback — Copy the same amount:**
The same amount does not create the same price, timing, or result.

**Incorrect Feedback — Borrow money before the price rises:**
Urgency does not remove the possibility of loss or repayment obligations.

**Incorrect Feedback — Assume the result will be identical:**
Another person’s successful result cannot guarantee yours.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A friend’s large profit means another person will achieve the same result.
