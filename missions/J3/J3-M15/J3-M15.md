# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J3-M15
**Journey:** Become an Investor
**Mission Title:** Stocks
**Core Concept:** A stock represents a small ownership share in a company. Owning more shares generally means owning a larger portion of that company.
**Target Age Group:** Teens 13–17
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand that buying a stock means purchasing partial ownership in a company.

**Primary Misconception:**
Stocks are only prices or numbers moving on a screen.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Owning one stock means owning the entire company.
* Buying shares guarantees control over the company or a profit.

**Key Takeaway:**
A stock represents a small ownership share in a company.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** A Share Is a Piece of a Business

**Explanation:**
You placed five of the company’s 100 shares into your ownership section. A **stock**, or share, represents partial ownership in a company. Your five shares equal 5% ownership in this simplified example. A stock price is what buyers and sellers may currently pay for that ownership piece. Owning shares does not mean owning the entire company, controlling every decision, or receiving guaranteed profit. The company’s results and market demand can affect what the shares are worth.

**Suggested Visual:**
The five ownership tiles connect the learner directly to the pizza shop, while a separate price tag moves up and down beside them.

**Explain It Simply:**
A stock is ownership in a business, not only a changing price.

**Primary Button Label:** See Another Example

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
A company has 200 equal shares. You own 10. What do those shares represent?

### Options

1. **5% ownership in the company**
2. Ownership of the entire company
3. A guaranteed yearly payment
4. Only ten numbers on a screen

**Correct Answer:**
5% ownership in the company

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Ten out of 200 equal shares is 5%. The shares represent partial ownership in the company.

**Incorrect Feedback — Ownership of the entire company:**
The other 190 shares belong to other owners, so you own only part of the company.

**Incorrect Feedback — A guaranteed yearly payment:**
Shares may produce gains or income, but neither is guaranteed.

**Incorrect Feedback — Only ten numbers on a screen:**
Prices may appear on a screen, but the shares represent ownership in a real company.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Stocks are only prices or numbers moving on a screen.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
You buy one share in a company with one million shares. What does that mean?

### Options

1. **You own a very small part of the company**
2. You own the entire company
3. You control every company decision
4. Your profit is guaranteed

**Correct Answer:**
You own a very small part of the company

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
One share represents partial ownership, but it is only one of the company’s one million ownership units.

**Incorrect Feedback — You own the entire company:**
Owning the entire company would require owning all of its shares.

**Incorrect Feedback — You control every company decision:**
One small share usually provides very limited influence. Voting rights can also vary.

**Incorrect Feedback — Your profit is guaranteed:**
Share prices and company results can rise or fall. A profit is never guaranteed.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Owning one stock means owning the entire company.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A company’s share price falls after disappointing sales. What is still true about each share?

### Options

1. **It still represents partial ownership, although its market value changed**
2. It stops representing the company
3. The owner is guaranteed the original price
4. The shareholder now controls the company

**Correct Answer:**
It still represents partial ownership, although its market value changed

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The market price may rise or fall, but the share continues to represent an ownership unit unless the company’s structure changes.

**Incorrect Feedback — It stops representing the company:**
A lower market price does not automatically remove the ownership represented by the share.

**Incorrect Feedback — The owner is guaranteed the original price:**
A shareholder may receive less than the purchase price if the share value falls.

**Incorrect Feedback — The shareholder now controls the company:**
A price change does not automatically change how many shares the person owns or give complete control.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Buying shares guarantees control over the company or a profit.
