# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J3-M16
**Journey:** Become an Investor
**Mission Title:** The Stock Market
**Core Concept:** The stock market connects buyers and sellers of company shares. Stock prices change when new information affects how much people are willing to pay or accept for those shares.
**Target Age Group:** Teens 13–17
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how the reactions and changing expectations of buyers and sellers influence stock prices.

**Primary Misconception:**
Stock prices move completely at random.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A stock price rises whenever a company makes a profit.
* One buyer or seller always controls the market price.

**Key Takeaway:**
Stock prices reflect changing expectations from buyers and sellers.

## Option 1

**Answer:** Buyers and sellers react to the information
**Recommended Answer:** Yes

**Response:**
Good thinking. People first update their expectations and change the prices they are willing to offer or accept.

## Option 2

**Answer:** The price automatically doubles

**Response:**
News does not create a fixed price change. Buyers and sellers may interpret the same information differently.

## Option 3

**Answer:** The company creates new shares immediately

**Response:**
New information does not automatically change the number of company shares.

## Option 4

**Answer:** One buyer permanently chooses the price

**Response:**
One order may affect a trade, but the market price usually reflects many buyers and sellers interacting.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Cause & Effect

**Interaction Title:** From News to Price

**Instruction:**
Arrange the four events in order. Then predict what may happen when buyers and sellers react differently.

## Educational Assumptions

* PixelForge Games is fictional.
* The activity uses a simplified market with a limited number of shares.
* The starting market price is approximately £10 per share.
* A market price is based on prices at which buyers and sellers agree to trade.
* “More buyers” means buying interest is stronger than the number of shares currently offered for sale.
* Price movements are possible outcomes, not guarantees.
* Real markets include many orders, different expectations, rapid changes, and additional factors.
* The activity does not recommend buying or selling any real stock.

## Events to Arrange

### Event Card A

**New information about the company appears.**

PixelForge announces strong pre-orders for its upcoming game.

### Event Card B

**Buyers and sellers react to the information.**

Some buyers become more hopeful and offer to buy. Some owners decide to keep their shares instead of selling.

### Event Card C

**Demand for the available shares changes.**

More people want to buy than the number of owners currently willing to sell.

### Event Card D

**The market price moves.**

Buyers may offer higher prices until a seller agrees to trade.

## Correct Sequence

1. New information about the company appears.
2. Buyers and sellers react to the information.
3. Demand for the available shares changes.
4. The market price moves.

## Cause-and-Effect Relationships

### Relationship 1

**Cause:** New information appears.
**Effect:** People update their expectations about the company’s future.

### Relationship 2

**Cause:** Expectations change.
**Effect:** Buyers and sellers change their orders or decide whether to trade.

### Relationship 3

**Cause:** Buying interest becomes stronger or weaker compared with selling interest.
**Effect:** Buyers and sellers adjust the prices they offer or accept.

### Relationship 4

**Cause:** A buyer and seller agree at a new price.
**Effect:** The latest market price may rise, fall, or remain similar.

## Scenario Check 1 — More Buyers Than Sellers

**Scenario:**
Many buyers want PixelForge shares, but few owners are willing to sell near £10.

**Question:**
What may happen next?

### Options

1. **Buyers may offer higher prices**
2. Sellers must accept lower prices
3. The price can never change
4. The company automatically creates more profit

**Likely Educational Outcome:**
Buyers may offer higher prices.

**Explanation:**
When several buyers compete for limited shares, some may raise their offers to persuade an owner to sell. This can push the market price higher.

## Scenario Check 2 — More Sellers Than Buyers

**Scenario:**
PixelForge delays its new game. More owners want to sell, while fewer buyers are interested at £10.

**Question:**
What may happen next?

### Options

1. Buyers must offer more
2. **Sellers may accept lower prices**

4. Every seller receives the original purchase price

**Likely Educational Outcome:**
Sellers may accept lower prices.

**Explanation:**
When many people want to sell but fewer people want to buy, sellers may lower their asking prices to find buyers. This can push the market price down.

## Correct Completion State

The learner must:

* Arrange the four events in the correct sequence.
* Identify a possible price rise when buying interest is stronger.
* Identify a possible price fall when selling interest is stronger.
* Identify relative stability when buying and selling interest are balanced.
* Recognise that these are possible outcomes, not guaranteed predictions.

## Alternative Contributing Factors

Stock prices may also be influenced by:

* Company earnings and future plans
* Product launches or delays
* Competitor news
* Changes in the wider economy
* Interest rates
* Industry developments
* New laws or regulations
* The overall mood of investors
* Rumours or incorrect information
* The number and size of buy and sell orders

## Explanation of Simplification

Real stock markets contain many buyers and sellers placing orders continuously. The market price commonly refers to the price of the most recent agreed trade.

One large order may affect the price, especially when few shares are being traded. However, one ordinary buyer or seller does not usually control the price permanently.

A price rise does not prove that a company is successful, and a current profit does not guarantee that the price will rise. Investors also compare real results with what they previously expected.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
A company announces stronger-than-expected customer growth. More people want to buy its limited shares than sell them. What may happen?

### Options

1. **Buyers may offer higher prices**
2. Sellers must lower the price
3. The price can never change
4. The company guarantees a profit

**Correct Answer:**
Buyers may offer higher prices

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
When buying interest is stronger than selling interest, buyers may compete by offering more. This can push the market price higher.

**Incorrect Feedback — Sellers must lower the price:**
Sellers are more likely to lower their prices when selling interest is stronger than buying interest.

**Incorrect Feedback — The price can never change:**
The price may change when buyers and sellers adjust the prices they will offer or accept.

**Incorrect Feedback — The company guarantees a profit:**
Higher demand for shares does not guarantee future company profits or investor gains.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Stock-price movements are unrelated to buyers, sellers, and new information.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Which statement about stock prices is most accurate?

### Options

1. **Prices respond to changing information and expectations, but remain difficult to predict**
2. Prices move completely at random
3. One buyer always controls the price
4. Every piece of good news makes the price rise

**Correct Answer:**
Prices respond to changing information and expectations, but remain difficult to predict

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Prices are influenced by buyers’ and sellers’ reactions to information. Different interpretations and unexpected events make future movements uncertain.

**Incorrect Feedback — Prices move completely at random:**
Price changes may be difficult to predict, but they are influenced by orders, information, expectations, and market conditions.

**Incorrect Feedback — One buyer always controls the price:**
Market prices usually result from the interaction of many buyers and sellers.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every piece of good news makes the price rise:**
The news may already be expected, or investors may interpret it differently.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Stock prices move completely at random.

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A company reports a profit, but the profit is much lower than investors expected. What could happen to its share price?

### Options

1. **It may fall because expectations were disappointed**
2. It must rise because the company made a profit
3. It must stay unchanged
4. One shareholder chooses the new price

**Correct Answer:**
It may fall because expectations were disappointed

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Investors compare results with earlier expectations. A profitable company’s share price may fall when its results are weaker than expected.

**Incorrect Feedback — It must rise because the company made a profit:**
A profit alone does not decide the price. Investors also consider whether the result was better or worse than expected.

**Incorrect Feedback — It must stay unchanged:**
New information may change what buyers and sellers are willing to offer or accept.

**Incorrect Feedback — One shareholder chooses the new price:**
The market price usually develops through trades between many buyers and sellers.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A stock price rises whenever a company makes a profit.
