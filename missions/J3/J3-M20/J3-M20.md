# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J3-M20
**Journey:** Become an Investor
**Mission Title:** Dividends
**Core Concept:** A dividend is a payment a company may choose to make to shareholders using part of its profits or available cash.
**Target Age Group:** Teens 13–17
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand that distributing dividends is one of several ways a company may use its profits.

**Primary Misconception:**
Every profitable company must pay dividends to its shareholders.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A company that does not pay dividends is automatically performing badly.
* Dividend payments are guaranteed and always remain the same.

**Key Takeaway:**
A dividend is one possible way a company shares profit with shareholders.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Cause & Effect

**Interaction Title:** Where Can the Profit Go?

**Instruction:**
Connect each company decision with its possible result. Then calculate what different shareholders may receive.

## Educational Assumptions

* BrightByte is a fictional company.
* The activity treats £10,000 as money available after normal operating costs.
* Real accounting profit and available cash are not always the same.
* The company has several possible uses for the money.
* Reinvestment may support growth but does not guarantee success.
* Debt repayment may reduce future interest costs, depending on the debt terms.
* The dividend example uses one ordinary share class with equal payments per eligible share.
* Taxes, fees, different share classes, and other company commitments are excluded.
* The activity does not recommend buying any real company’s shares.

## Company Decisions

### Decision A

**Reinvest the profit**

### Decision B

**Keep the profit as savings**

### Decision C

**Repay debt**

### Decision D

**Distribute some profit as dividends**

## Possible Results

### Result 1

The company may improve products, hire employees, open locations, or expand operations.

### Result 2

The company builds a cash reserve for future opportunities or unexpected costs.

### Result 3

The company reduces the amount it owes and may lower future interest costs.

### Result 4

Eligible shareholders receive a payment based on how many shares they own.

## Correct Connections

### Reinvest the Profit → Support Future Growth

**Explanation:**
The company uses the money inside the business. This may help it grow, but success is not guaranteed.

### Keep the Profit as Savings → Build a Cash Reserve

**Explanation:**
The company keeps money available for future plans or unexpected costs.

### Repay Debt → Reduce Money Owed

**Explanation:**
Repaying debt lowers the outstanding balance and may reduce future interest costs.

### Distribute Dividends → Pay Eligible Shareholders

**Explanation:**
The company shares some money with eligible shareholders according to the dividend declared per share.

---

## Incorrect Connection Feedback

### Reinvestment Matched With Shareholder Payment

Reinvested money stays inside the company rather than being distributed to shareholders.

### Reinvestment Matched With Cash Reserve

A reserve remains available as savings. Reinvestment puts the money into business activities.

### Reinvestment Matched With Debt Reduction

Reinvestment funds company operations or growth rather than directly reducing debt.

### Savings Matched With Business Expansion

Saving the money builds a reserve. Expansion would require the company to use or reinvest it.

### Savings Matched With Debt Reduction

Money kept as savings remains available rather than being used to repay what the company owes.

### Savings Matched With Shareholder Payment

Money distributed to shareholders leaves the company instead of remaining in its reserve.

### Debt Repayment Matched With Expansion

Debt repayment reduces money owed rather than directly funding new products or locations.

### Debt Repayment Matched With Cash Reserve

Money used to repay debt is no longer being kept as available savings.

### Debt Repayment Matched With Shareholder Payment

Debt repayment goes to lenders. Dividends are paid to eligible shareholders.

### Dividends Matched With Expansion

Dividend money is distributed to shareholders rather than reinvested in the company.

### Dividends Matched With Cash Reserve

A dividend reduces cash held by the company instead of building its reserve.

### Dividends Matched With Debt Reduction

Dividends go to eligible shareholders, while debt repayments go to lenders.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** One Choice Among Several

**Explanation:**
You connected company profit with four possible uses. A **dividend** is a payment a company may choose to make to eligible shareholders, often stated as an amount per share. A profitable company does not have to pay one. It may reinvest, build savings, or repay debt instead. A company can also split its money between several choices. Dividends may increase, decrease, remain unchanged, or stop, so shareholders should never treat them as guaranteed income.

**Suggested Visual:**
A company-profit card divides across growth, savings, debt, and dividend paths, with no path marked as compulsory.

**Explain It Simply:**
A dividend is optional and depends on the company’s decision.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
A company declares a dividend of £0.50 per share. Liam owns eight eligible shares. How much does he receive?

### Options

1. £0.50
2. £2
3. **£4**
4. £8

**Correct Answer:**
£4

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Eight shares multiplied by £0.50 per share equals £4.

**Incorrect Feedback — £0.50:**
£0.50 is the payment for one share. Liam owns eight shares.

**Incorrect Feedback — £2:**
Four shares would receive £2. Eight shares receive twice that amount.

**Incorrect Feedback — £8:**
£8 would mean a dividend of £1 per share rather than £0.50.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A dividend payment is unrelated to the number of eligible shares owned.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
A profitable company pays no dividend and uses the money to develop a new product. What does this show?

### Options

1. **Profitable companies may choose to reinvest instead**
2. The company must be failing
3. Every shareholder has lost their shares
4. Paying no dividend is illegal

**Correct Answer:**
Profitable companies may choose to reinvest instead

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
A dividend is optional. A company may decide that investing in its operations is a better use of the money.

**Incorrect Feedback — The company must be failing:**
A profitable company may intentionally keep or reinvest money to support future plans.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every shareholder has lost their shares:**
Not receiving a dividend does not remove the shareholder’s ownership.

**Incorrect Feedback — Paying no dividend is illegal:**
Companies are not automatically required to distribute dividends whenever they earn a profit.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Every profitable company must pay dividends to its shareholders.

---

**Incorrect Feedback — It must increase:**
Dividend payments can increase, but they can also decrease or stop.

**Incorrect Feedback — Profit guarantees a payment:**

**Misconception Being Tested:**
