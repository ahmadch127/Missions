# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J3-M18
**Journey:** Become an Investor
**Mission Title:** ETFs
**Core Concept:** An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, allows an investor to buy access to a basket of investments through a single purchase.
**Target Age Group:** Teens 13–17
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how an ETF can provide access to multiple investments instead of requiring the investor to buy each one separately.

**Primary Misconception:**
ETFs and individual stocks are identical.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Every ETF contains the same investments.
* Buying an ETF guarantees protection from losses.

**Key Takeaway:**
An ETF can provide access to a basket of investments through one purchase.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Match Pairs

**Interaction Title:** Stock, ETF, or Index?

**Instruction:**
Match each investing term with its correct meaning.

## Educational Assumptions

* All companies and investments in this activity are fictional.
* A single stock represents ownership in one company.
* An ETF is a fund whose shares can be bought and sold through a market.
* An ETF may contain company shares, bonds, or other investments.
* Many ETFs follow a market index, but ETFs can follow different strategies.
* Investors own shares of the ETF rather than directly owning each underlying investment.
* Different ETFs contain different holdings and may charge different fees.
* Holding several investments may spread risk but cannot prevent every loss.
* The activity does not recommend buying any real ETF or stock.

## Left-Side Items

1. Single stock
2. ETF
3. Market index

## Right-Side Items

1. Ownership in one company
2. A basket of investments accessed through one purchase
3. A selected group used to measure investment performance

## Correct Pairs

### Single Stock → Ownership in One Company

**Explanation:**
A stock is a small ownership share in one company. Its result depends heavily on that company’s performance and market price.

### ETF → A Basket of Investments Accessed Through One Purchase

**Explanation:**
An ETF combines multiple investments inside one fund. Buying an ETF share gives access to that basket.

### Market Index → A Selected Group Used to Measure Investment Performance

**Explanation:**
An index tracks the performance of a chosen group. Some ETFs are designed to follow that group.

## Feedback for Incorrect Matching

### Single Stock Matched With Basket of Investments

A single stock normally represents one company. An ETF may contain a basket of different investments.

### Single Stock Matched With Market Index

A stock is ownership in a company. An index measures the performance of a selected group.

### ETF Matched With Ownership in One Company

An ETF is a fund that may hold several investments. A single stock represents one company.

### ETF Matched With Market Index

An ETF may follow an index, but they are not identical. The ETF is the fund; the index is the group or measurement it follows.

### Market Index Matched With Ownership in One Company

An index is not ownership in one company. It measures a selected group of investments.

### Market Index Matched With Basket Through One Purchase

An index describes or measures a group. An ETF is one way an investor may gain access to a basket based on that group.

## Risk Check

**Question:**
If several companies inside the ETF lose value, what may happen?

### Options

1. **The ETF’s value may also fall**
2. The ETF must stay at the same price
3. The fund manager must repay every loss
4. The profitable companies guarantee a positive result

**Correct Answer:**
The ETF’s value may also fall

**Explanation:**
Spreading money across several holdings can reduce dependence on one company, but the whole basket can still lose value.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which purchase is most likely to provide access to several companies at once?

### Options

1. **One share of an ETF holding 50 companies**
2. One share of a single company
3. A gift card
4. One company bond only

**Correct Answer:**
One share of an ETF holding 50 companies

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The ETF contains a basket of 50 company holdings, so one ETF purchase provides access to the combined portfolio.

**Incorrect Feedback — One share of a single company:**
A single stock normally represents ownership in one company.

**Incorrect Feedback — A gift card:**
A gift card provides spending credit, not access to an investment portfolio.

**Incorrect Feedback — One company bond only:**
That purchase provides exposure to one bond issuer rather than a basket of companies.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
An ETF and a single-company investment provide identical exposure.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Which statement correctly compares an ETF with an individual stock?

### Options

1. **An ETF may hold many investments; a stock represents one company**
2. They are always identical
3. Every ETF owns the same companies
4. Both guarantee a profit

**Correct Answer:**
An ETF may hold many investments; a stock represents one company

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
A single stock is ownership in one company. An ETF is a fund that may contain many different holdings.

**Incorrect Feedback — They are always identical:**
Both may be traded through markets, but they represent different investment structures.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every ETF owns the same companies:**
ETFs follow different indices or strategies and can contain very different holdings.

**Incorrect Feedback — Both guarantee a profit:**
Stocks and ETFs can both fall in value.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
ETFs and individual stocks are identical.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
An ETF holds 100 companies, but most are in the same struggling industry. What should an investor understand?

### Options

1. **Many holdings do not remove risk when they face similar problems**
2. The ETF cannot lose money
3. One hundred holdings guarantee diversification
4. The manager must repay any fall

**Correct Answer:**
Many holdings do not remove risk when they face similar problems

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The number of holdings matters, but their similarities also matter. Companies in the same industry may fall together.

**Incorrect Feedback — The ETF cannot lose money:**
Every ETF can lose value when its underlying holdings fall.

**Incorrect Feedback — One hundred holdings guarantee diversification:**
A fund can hold many investments that are still exposed to the same industry or risk.

**Incorrect Feedback — The manager must repay any fall:**
Fund managers manage the portfolio but do not guarantee or personally repay investment losses.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Buying an ETF guarantees protection from losses.
