## Learning Objective

Apply ownership, lending, pooled investing, risk, time horizon, and cash-access concepts to build a portfolio suited to a long-term goal.

## Primary Combined Misconception

A strong portfolio is simply the one with the highest possible growth potential.

## Secondary Misconceptions

- Every investor should choose the same portfolio.
- Holding several individual stocks always creates a diversified portfolio.
- Cash has no useful role because it does not usually offer high growth.
- A company that does not pay dividends is automatically a poor investment.

## Key Takeaway

**A strong portfolio fits the goal, timeline, cash needs, diversification, and ability to accept risk.**

# 2. Boss Mission Overview

The learner builds a Rs. 100,000 portfolio for a five-year goal. First, they describe the goal timeline, reaction to a possible decline, and need for accessible cash. Next, they allocate money across stocks, bonds, a mutual fund, an ETF, and cash. They then explain each asset’s role and predict how a company may use profit. The final portfolio check responds to the learner’s earlier profile and allocation. Results measure concentration, access, risk, alignment, understanding, and decision quality—not luck.

## Situation

Before allocating the Rs. 100,000, answer three simple questions about when the money may be needed, how a 20% decline might feel, and how much should remain accessible.

## Learner Objective

Create an educational profile that guides later feedback without producing a formal suitability label.

## Decision Tree Configuration

### When might the portfolio money be needed?

- Within one year
- In two to five years
- More than five years from now
- I am not sure
### How might you react if the portfolio fell 20%?

- Sell everything immediately
- Feel worried but review the plan
- Accept it as long-term uncertainty
- I do not know
### Do you need quick access to some of the Rs. 100,000?

- Yes, a large part may be needed soon
- Yes, a small amount should stay accessible
- No, most can remain invested
- I am not sure

### Profile Feedback Rules

- **time_horizon_equals_within_one_year:** More money may need to remain accessible and less exposed to sharp price movement.
- **time_horizon_equals_more_than_five_years:** A longer timeline may allow more time for price changes, but losses remain possible.
- **decline_reaction_equals_sell_immediately:** A concentrated stock portfolio may be emotionally difficult to maintain during declines.
- **access_need_equals_large_access_need:** A meaningful cash allocation may be useful because selling investments at the wrong time can create pressure.
- **any_profile_answer_equals_unsure:** Uncertainty is useful information. A more moderate starting allocation may be easier to review.

**Important Clarification:** These answers guide educational feedback only. They do not create a professional risk label or recommendation.

## Available Choices or Completion States

### Complete all three profile questions honestly

**Classification:** Recommended

**Immediate Financial or State Effect:** The portfolio builder can now compare the allocation with the selected timeline, reaction, and cash needs.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Profile considerations appear beside the builder.

**Possible Later Consequence:** Alignment, access, and concentration feedback becomes context-sensitive.

## Recommended Decision Logic

**Generally Strongest:** Choose the answers that best describe the fictional situation rather than choosing the option that sounds most adventurous.

**Other Reasonable Paths:**

- A shorter timeline may justify more cash or lower-movement holdings.
- A longer timeline may support more growth exposure when possible losses are acceptable.
- An unsure learner may reasonably begin with a moderate spread and review it later.

**Trade-Off:** More growth exposure may increase price movement, while more cash improves access but usually lowers growth potential.

# Stage 4 — Company Profit Decision

## Stage Metadata

**Stage ID:** J3-BM3-ST4  
**Timeline:** Year One Review  
**Interaction:** Predict Outcome Decision Tree  
**Concepts Assessed:** dividends, company_profit_decisions, portfolio_alignment, diversification, cash_access

## Situation

A company held directly or inside a fund earns a profit. Predict the trade-offs of four profit choices. Then use your profile and current allocation to decide whether the portfolio needs one final adjustment.

## Learner Objective

Understand that dividends are optional and complete a context-sensitive final portfolio check.

## Predict Outcome Configuration

### Pay a dividend

- Eligible shareholders may receive cash, while the company keeps less for other uses **(Likely educational outcome)**
- The company guarantees future growth
- Every shareholder avoids all losses

**Explanation:** A dividend may provide shareholder cash, but it leaves less money for expansion, debt repayment, or reserves.

### Reinvest in growth

- The company may expand, but the growth result is uncertain **(Likely educational outcome)**
- Shareholders must receive an immediate payment
- The company can no longer lose money

**Explanation:** Reinvestment may support products, workers, or expansion, but useful growth and future returns are not guaranteed.

### Repay debt

- The company may lower what it owes, leaving less for dividends or expansion **(Likely educational outcome)**
- The company automatically becomes risk-free
- Shareholders immediately own a larger percentage

**Explanation:** Debt repayment may strengthen the company, but other uses of profit receive less money.

### Keep cash available

- The company gains flexibility, but too much unused cash may limit growth **(Likely educational outcome)**
- The cash guarantees a dividend later
- The cash cannot lose purchasing power

**Explanation:** Cash reserves can support emergencies or opportunities, but holding too much unused cash can have a trade-off.

**Important Clarification:** There is no single correct use of company profit. The strongest choice depends on debt, growth opportunities, cash needs, risks, and strategy.

## Final Portfolio Check

What should happen before the portfolio begins its five-year journey?

Dynamic recommendations may appear for high concentration, low cash access, near-term risk mismatch, or no major mismatch.

## Available Choices or Completion States

### Keep the portfolio and schedule a later review

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Financial or State Effect:** Keeping the allocation may be reasonable when it already matches the timeline, access needs, and concentration limits.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Allocations stay unchanged.

**Possible Later Consequence:** The learner must still accept that values can rise or fall.

### Reduce the largest allocation by Rs. 10,000 and spread it elsewhere

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Financial or State Effect:** This reduces dependence on the largest category when concentration is high.

**Short-Term Consequence:** The largest allocation falls and another suitable category increases.

**Possible Later Consequence:** Concentration may improve, but losses remain possible.

### Move Rs. 10,000 from investments to cash

**Classification:** Reasonable

**Immediate Financial or State Effect:** This improves access when the current cash allocation does not match the selected need.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Cash rises by Rs. 10,000 and growth exposure falls.

**Possible Later Consequence:** The portfolio may be easier to use for a near-term need but may have lower growth potential.

### Move all bonds and cash into individual stocks for maximum growth potential

**Classification:** Risky

**Immediate Financial or State Effect:** The portfolio chases growth by increasing concentration and reducing accessible money.

**Short-Term Consequence:** Individual-stock exposure rises sharply.

**Possible Later Consequence:** A decline may be harder to tolerate and cash may be unavailable when needed.

## Recommended Decision Logic

**Generally Strongest:** Use the portfolio’s actual mismatch: reduce concentration, improve cash access, or keep the allocation when it already fits.

**Other Reasonable Paths:**

- Keeping the portfolio is reasonable when no major mismatch appears.
- Reducing concentration is reasonable when one category dominates.
- Increasing cash is reasonable when the learner expects to need money soon.

**Trade-Off:** Improving access or reducing concentration may lower growth potential, while chasing growth may create a harder portfolio to maintain.

# 14. Concept Reveal and Debrief

**Debrief Title:** Fit Matters More Than Maximum Growth
