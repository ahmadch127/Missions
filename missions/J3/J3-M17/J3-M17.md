# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J3-M17
**Journey:** Become an Investor
**Mission Title:** Mutual Funds
**Core Concept:** A mutual fund combines money from many investors into one professionally managed pool that can hold shares, bonds, or other investments.
**Target Age Group:** Teens 13–17
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how a mutual fund pools money from multiple investors to purchase a collection of investments.

**Primary Misconception:**
A mutual fund is one company.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Every investor directly owns each investment held by the fund.
* A professionally managed mutual fund cannot lose value.

**Key Takeaway:**
A mutual fund pools money from many investors.

**Interaction Type:** Drag & Drop

**Interaction Title:** Pool It, Then Invest It

**Instruction:**
First combine the learners’ contributions. Then place the four investments inside the mutual fund portfolio.

## Educational Assumptions

* This is a fictional classroom activity.
* One hundred learners contribute a combined £1,000.
* Contributions vary between learners.
* The activity creates one fund unit for every £1 contributed.
* The fund therefore has 1,000 units in total.
* A professional fund manager follows a stated classroom strategy.
* Investment values may rise or fall.
* Professional management and holding several investments do not guarantee profit.
* No fees, taxes, trading costs, or currency changes are included.
* The activity does not recommend a real mutual fund or investment.

## What the Investors Own

The learners own units of the mutual fund:

* Learner A owns 10 fund units.
* Learner B owns 20 fund units.
* Learner C owns 15 fund units.
* The other learners collectively own 955 fund units.

They do not directly receive separate technology shares, food-company shares, bonds, or transport-company shares.

The fund owns the underlying investments on behalf of its investors.

---

## Role of the Fund Manager

The professional fund manager:

* Follows the fund’s stated strategy
* Chooses and monitors investments
* Decides how the pooled money is allocated
* May buy or sell investments when appropriate
* Cannot guarantee that the fund will make money

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** Many Investors, One Managed Pool

**Explanation:**
You combined money from 100 learners and used the shared pool to buy four investments. This is how a **mutual fund** works: many investors contribute money to one professionally managed fund. Investors own units or shares of the fund, while the fund holds the underlying investments. Holding several investments can spread risk, but it cannot remove risk. Professional management also does not guarantee a profit, and the value of fund units can rise or fall.

**Suggested Visual:**
Many contribution arrows enter one mutual fund, which branches into four investments. Fund-unit certificates return to the investors.

**Explain It Simply:**
Investors own fund units; the fund owns the investment collection.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which situation best describes a mutual fund?

### Options

1. **Many investors combine money into one managed investment pool**
2. One person buys one company
3. A company gives guaranteed returns
4. Investors store cash in separate envelopes

**Correct Answer:**
Many investors combine money into one managed investment pool

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
A mutual fund pools contributions from many investors and uses the combined money to hold a collection of investments.

**Incorrect Feedback — One person buys one company:**
That is a direct investment in one company, not a pooled mutual fund.

**Incorrect Feedback — A company gives guaranteed returns:**
Mutual funds can rise or fall in value and cannot guarantee returns.

**Incorrect Feedback — Investors store cash in separate envelopes:**
A mutual fund combines the money rather than keeping every contribution completely separate.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A mutual fund is simply one direct company investment.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Is a mutual fund one company?

### Options

1. **No, it is a fund that may hold investments in several companies or assets**
2. Yes, every mutual fund is one company share
3. Yes, because one manager runs it
4. Yes, if it owns technology shares

**Correct Answer:**
No, it is a fund that may hold investments in several companies or assets

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
A mutual fund is a pooled investment structure. Its portfolio may contain shares, bonds, or other investments.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, every mutual fund is one company share:**
One company share represents ownership in one company. A mutual fund can hold many different investments.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, because one manager runs it:**
Professional management does not turn the fund into one company.

**Incorrect Feedback — Yes, if it owns technology shares:**
Technology shares may be only one part of a larger fund portfolio.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A mutual fund is one company.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A mutual fund holds four investments. Two rise in value and two fall. What can happen to the fund?

### Options

1. **Its total value depends on the combined results of all four investments**
2. It must make a profit
3. It cannot lose value because it is professionally managed
4. Every investor directly owns each company share

**Correct Answer:**
Its total value depends on the combined results of all four investments

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The fund’s total value reflects the combined performance of its holdings after relevant costs. Gains in some holdings may offset losses in others, but not always.

**Incorrect Feedback — It must make a profit:**
Several investments may spread risk, but they cannot guarantee a positive result.

**Incorrect Feedback — It cannot lose value because it is professionally managed:**
Professional managers can make decisions, but they cannot control markets or prevent every loss.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every investor directly owns each company share:**
Investors generally own units or shares of the fund, while the fund holds the underlying investments.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A professionally managed mutual fund cannot lose value.
