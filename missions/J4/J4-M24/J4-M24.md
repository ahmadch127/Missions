# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J4-M24
**Journey:** Grow Wealth Wisely
**Mission Title:** Start Early
**Core Concept:** Starting to invest earlier gives money more time to grow and potentially earn returns on previous growth.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how starting earlier can improve long-term investment outcomes by giving money more time to grow.

**Primary Misconception:**
Starting later with more money always makes it easy to catch up with someone who started earlier.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* Small amounts invested early cannot make a meaningful difference.
* The amount invested matters, but the length of time invested does not.

**Key Takeaway:**
Time can be one of the strongest advantages in long-term investing.

**Instruction:**

**Starting age:** 20

## Slider Steps

1. Age 20
2. Age 30
3. Age 40

5. Age 60

## Educational Assumptions

* Hana starts contributing at age 20.
* Omar starts contributing at age 30.
* Each contributes £100 at the end of every month after starting.
* Both continue until age 60.
* The activity uses an illustrative annual return of 5%, compounded monthly.
* The return remains smooth and constant only to make the role of time easy to see.
* Real returns do not arrive smoothly and may be positive or negative.
* Fees, taxes, inflation, missed contributions, and currency changes are excluded.
* Balances are rounded to the nearest pound.
* These values are examples, not forecasts or guaranteed outcomes.
* The activity is educational and does not recommend a real investment.

## Cause-and-Effect Feedback

### Start Earlier With a Smaller Amount

**Possible effect:**
The money receives more time to potentially grow.

### Wait Ten Years Before Starting

**Possible effect:**
There are fewer years for contributions and potential compound growth.

### Invest More Money Later

**Possible effect:**
Larger contributions may help close the gap, but catching up may require much more money.

### Pause Contributions During a Market Decline

**Possible effect:**
Less money is contributed during that period, and the investor may miss part of a later recovery.

**Clarification:**
Personal cash needs may still require contribution changes. Continuing is not always possible or appropriate.

The slider must always display these amounts separately:

* Total illustrative balance

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** Time Gives Growth More Chances

**Explanation:**
You saw Hana’s contributions begin working ten years sooner. This creates more time for **compound growth**, where returns may generate further returns. Starting early does not guarantee profit, and actual results depend on investment performance, fees, taxes, contribution consistency, and time invested. However, a longer time horizon can make modest contributions more powerful. Waiting may still be recoverable, but catching up can require much larger payments or a later goal date.

**Suggested Visual:**
Early contributions grow into branches, with later growth forming additional branches over a 40-year timeline.

**Explain It Simply:**
Starting earlier gives contributions and potential growth more time to build.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Two people contribute £100 monthly until age 60. One starts at 22 and the other at 32. What advantage does the first person have?

### Options

1. A guaranteed profit
2. A higher monthly contribution
3. **Ten additional years for contributions and potential growth**
4. Protection from every market decline

**Correct Answer:**
Ten additional years for contributions and potential growth

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The earlier investor contributes for longer, and each early contribution has more time to potentially grow.

**Incorrect Feedback — A guaranteed profit:**
Starting early provides more time, but it cannot guarantee positive returns.

**Incorrect Feedback — A higher monthly contribution:**
Both people contribute the same monthly amount.

**Incorrect Feedback — Protection from every market decline:**
An early investor can still experience losses and volatility.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
The length of time invested does not affect the outcome.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Someone starts ten years later but contributes more each month. What is most accurate?

### Options

1. Catching up is always quick and easy
2. The larger contribution guarantees a better result
3. Starting time no longer matters
4. **Larger contributions may help, but catching up can require much more money**

**Correct Answer:**
Larger contributions may help, but catching up can require much more money

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The later investor has fewer years for contributions and potential growth, so closing the gap may require a substantially larger monthly amount.

**Incorrect Feedback — Catching up is always quick and easy:**
The required increase may be significant, especially when the earlier money has grown for many years.

**Incorrect Feedback — The larger contribution guarantees a better result:**
Investment returns remain uncertain regardless of the amount contributed.

**Incorrect Feedback — Starting time no longer matters:**
A larger payment does not restore the years that have already passed.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Starting later with more money always makes it easy to catch up.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A person can contribute £25 monthly now or wait ten years before contributing £100. What does starting now provide?

### Options

1. Guaranteed wealth
2. A guaranteed better final result
3. **More time for the early contributions to potentially grow**
4. Protection from fees and taxes

**Correct Answer:**
More time for the early contributions to potentially grow

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Even a small early amount receives additional time. The final result still depends on performance, fees, consistency, and later decisions.

**Incorrect Feedback — Guaranteed wealth:**
Starting early can help, but no contribution schedule guarantees wealth.

**Incorrect Feedback — A guaranteed better final result:**
The later contribution is larger, and actual returns are uncertain. The early option’s clear advantage is time.

**Incorrect Feedback — Protection from fees and taxes:**
Starting time does not remove fees or tax considerations.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Small amounts invested early cannot make a meaningful difference.
