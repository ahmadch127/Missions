# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J4-M23
**Journey:** Grow Wealth Wisely
**Mission Title:** Volatility
**Core Concept:** Volatility describes how much and how quickly an investment’s price moves up and down over time.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 4 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** United Kingdom — GBP (£), en-GB
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand that short-term price movement can differ greatly between investments, even when their long-term results are similar.

**Primary Misconception:**
Any drop in an investment’s price means the investment is bad or has permanently failed.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* A less volatile investment is always a better investment.
* A highly volatile investment will always produce a higher long-term return.

**Key Takeaway:**
Volatility describes price movement, not necessarily permanent failure.

# 5. Main Interaction

**Interaction Type:** Before vs After

**Interaction Title:** Compare the Path, Not Just the Finish

**Instruction:**
Compare both investments before, during, and after five years. Identify which one moved more and what its temporary declines mean.

## Educational Assumptions

* Both investments are fictional.
* Each investment begins at £1,000.
* The comparison lasts five years.
* No fees, taxes, dividends, interest, inflation, or currency changes are included.
* The displayed values are teaching examples, not market forecasts.
* A temporary price decline may be followed by recovery, further decline, or little change.
* A similar ending value does not mean two investments carried the same experience or risk.
* Lower volatility does not guarantee a positive return.
* Higher volatility does not guarantee a higher return.
* The activity does not recommend buying, holding, or selling any real investment.

## Hidden Difference

The final values alone hide the journey.

An investor looking only at Year 5 might conclude that the investments were nearly identical. The yearly values reveal that Investment B involved much larger movements and greater short-term uncertainty.

---

## Cause-and-Effect Check 1

**Event:**
An investment falls temporarily.

**Possible result:**
Its price may recover, remain low, or fall further.

### Question

What can be concluded immediately from the fall?

#### Options

1. The investment is permanently worthless
2. It will definitely recover
3. **Its future result remains uncertain**
4. It must finish with a profit

**Correct Answer:**
Its future result remains uncertain

**Explanation:**
A temporary fall does not prove permanent failure, but it also does not guarantee recovery.

## Cause-and-Effect Check 2

**Event:**
An investor sells Investment B at £850 after buying at £1,000.

**Possible result:**

3. It is automatically refunded
4. It guarantees the next investment will rise

**Correct Answer:**
It becomes a realised £150 loss

**Explanation:**
Selling at £850 after investing £1,000 locks in the £150 difference, excluding fees and taxes.

## Important Clarification

Volatility describes the size and frequency of price movements. By itself, it does not tell an investor:

* Whether an investment is good or bad
* Whether its price will recover
* Whether its underlying value has permanently changed
* Whether it will produce a higher return
* Whether it is suitable for a particular person
* Whether selling or holding is the correct decision

Volatility should be considered alongside:

* Investment quality
* Time horizon
* Financial goals
* Risk of permanent loss
* Diversification
* Need for access to the money
* Ability to tolerate temporary declines

* Lower volatility does not guarantee a higher return or no losses.
* Higher volatility does not guarantee a better long-term result.
* Similar final values can hide very different journeys.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which price path shows greater volatility?

### Options

1. £1,000 → £1,020 → £1,040 → £1,060
2. **£1,000 → £1,250 → £800 → £1,100**
3. £1,000 → £1,010 → £1,015 → £1,020
4. £1,000 → £1,000 → £1,005 → £1,010

**Correct Answer:**
£1,000 → £1,250 → £800 → £1,100

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
This path has the largest and quickest movements in both directions.

**Incorrect Feedback — £1,000 → £1,020 → £1,040 → £1,060:**
This path rises in relatively small, steady steps.

**Incorrect Feedback — £1,000 → £1,010 → £1,015 → £1,020:**
These movements are small compared with the sharp rise and fall in the correct answer.

**Incorrect Feedback — £1,000 → £1,000 → £1,005 → £1,010:**
This path changes very little during the displayed period.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Volatility is determined only by whether the final value is higher or lower.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
An investment falls by 15% this month. What can you conclude?

### Options

1. It has permanently failed
2. It will definitely recover
3. It is automatically a bad investment
4. **Its price fell, but its future outcome remains uncertain**

**Correct Answer:**
Its price fell, but its future outcome remains uncertain

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The decline shows a change in price. More information is needed to judge the investment’s condition and future prospects.

**Incorrect Feedback — It has permanently failed:**
A temporary decline does not automatically prove permanent failure.

**Incorrect Feedback — It will definitely recover:**
Some investments recover, while others remain low or fall further.

**Incorrect Feedback — It is automatically a bad investment:**
Price movement alone does not provide enough information to judge overall quality.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Any price drop means an investment is bad or has permanently failed.

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
Two investments both gain 20% over five years. One experiences several 30% declines. What should an investor recognise?

### Options

1. Both had identical journeys
2. The more volatile investment guaranteed a higher return
3. **The final returns were similar, but the short-term experiences were different**
4. The steadier investment was automatically better

**Correct Answer:**
The final returns were similar, but the short-term experiences were different

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
The more volatile investment exposed the investor to larger temporary declines even though the final gain was similar.

**Incorrect Feedback — Both had identical journeys:**
The final percentages may match while the paths differ greatly.

**Incorrect Feedback — The more volatile investment guaranteed a higher return:**
Higher volatility does not guarantee a better long-term result.

**Incorrect Feedback — The steadier investment was automatically better:**
Lower volatility may be easier to tolerate, but suitability and quality depend on more than price movement.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
A less volatile investment is always better, or high volatility always produces higher returns.
