# Part A — Human-Readable Mission Document

# 1. Mission Metadata

**Mission ID:** J4-M25
**Journey:** Grow Wealth Wisely
**Mission Title:** Regular Investing
**Core Concept:** Regular investing means contributing a planned amount at consistent intervals instead of waiting for a supposedly perfect market entry point.
**Target Age Group:** Adults 18+
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Estimated Duration:** 5 minutes
**Currency and Locale:** Pakistan — PKR (Rs.), en-PK
**Language:** English

**Learning Objective:**
Understand how investing consistently over time can reduce the pressure and uncertainty of trying to predict the perfect moment to invest.

**Primary Misconception:**
Successful investing requires accurately predicting the best time to enter the market.

**Secondary Misconceptions:**

* An investor should stop contributing whenever market prices fall.
* Regular investing guarantees a profit or prevents losses.

**Key Takeaway:**
Regular investing reduces the pressure of trying to predict the perfect time.

**Fictional Market Fund**

---

## Educational Assumptions

* The simulation lasts twelve months.
* Ayesha automatically invests Rs. 5,000 every month.
* Hamza begins with Rs. 60,000 in cash.
* Each month, the learner chooses Hamza’s action.
* Units may be purchased in fractions.
* The final comparison uses the Month 12 unit price of Rs. 118.
* Cash does not earn interest during this simplified activity.
* Fees, taxes, inflation, dividends, and currency changes are excluded.
* Prices are fictional and selected to demonstrate changing purchase prices.
* The lowest price is visible only after the year is complete.
* Regular investing may produce gains or losses.
* Investing everything at once may perform better or worse than regular investing.
* The simulator does not recommend either strategy for a particular person.

## Required Disclaimer

**This is an educational simulation. The prices and outcomes are fictional and do not predict or guarantee real investment performance.**

## Learner Choices for Hamza

At each month, the learner may choose:

### Invest Rs. 5,000

Invest Rs. 5,000 from Hamza’s remaining cash at the current unit price.

### Wait

Keep all remaining money in cash and review the market again next month.

### Invest All Remaining Cash

Invest Hamza’s entire remaining cash balance at the current unit price.

### Stop for the Rest of the Year

Make no further purchases. Any remaining money stays in cash until the simulation ends.

Once all remaining cash has been invested or the learner stops, the simulator continues displaying prices without requiring further investment decisions.

# Strategy Comparison

| Strategy                  | Decision Pressure | Dependence on One Entry Price | Possible Outcome                |
| ------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Regular monthly investing | Lower             | Lower                         | Buys at a range of prices       |
| One lump-sum entry        | Higher            | Higher                        | May outperform or underperform  |
| Wait indefinitely         | Ongoing           | No market entry               | May miss gains and avoid losses |

## Important Hidden Difference

The simulation is not designed to prove that regular investing always earns the most.

A perfectly timed lump-sum purchase performed better in this fictional year. However, identifying the perfect month required future knowledge that was unavailable at the time.

Regular investing reduced dependence on making that prediction.

## Wait for the Lowest Price

**Possible effect:**

## Continue During a Price Decline

**Possible effect:**
The same contribution purchases more units, although the price may continue falling.

## Stop Whenever Prices Fall

**Possible effect:**
Short-term fear may interrupt the plan, and the investor may miss part of a later recovery.

## Invest Everything at Once

**Possible effect:**
The result depends heavily on one entry price. This may produce a better or worse outcome than regular contributions.

# 6. Concept Reveal

**Reveal Title:** A Plan Instead of a Prediction

**Explanation:**
You saw how **regular investing** spreads purchases across several prices. The same contribution buys more units when prices are lower and fewer when prices are higher. This reduces dependence on correctly guessing one perfect entry point. It does not guarantee a profit, the lowest average price, or protection from losses. A lump-sum investment may perform better or worse. Regular investing mainly provides a consistent process that can reduce market-timing pressure and emotional decision-making.

**Suggested Visual:**
Twelve smaller purchases form a path across changing prices while one large purchase waits beside an uncertain market chart.

**Explain It Simply:**
Regular contributions reduce reliance on one perfectly timed purchase.

# 8. Quiz

## Question 1 — Recognition

**Question:**
Which behaviour best represents regular investing?

### Options

1. Investing only after a large market rise
2. Waiting indefinitely for the lowest price
3. **Contributing a planned amount at consistent intervals**
4. Investing only when online comments are positive

**Correct Answer:**
Contributing a planned amount at consistent intervals

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Regular investing follows a contribution schedule rather than requiring a prediction about the perfect market entry.

**Incorrect Feedback — Investing only after a large market rise:**
This reacts to price movement rather than following a consistent plan.

**Incorrect Feedback — Waiting indefinitely for the lowest price:**
The lowest point may not be identifiable until it has already passed.

**Incorrect Feedback — Investing only when online comments are positive:**
Online sentiment can change quickly and does not create a consistent investment process.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Regular investing means reacting to short-term market signals.

---

## Question 2 — Misconception

**Question:**
Does successful investing require predicting the market’s exact lowest point?

### Options

1. Yes, every successful investor finds the bottom
2. Yes, otherwise profit is impossible
3. **No, regular contributions can reduce reliance on one prediction**
4. No, because prices never fall

**Correct Answer:**
No, regular contributions can reduce reliance on one prediction

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
Investing at several points avoids making the entire result depend on one perfectly timed purchase.

**Incorrect Feedback — Every successful investor finds the bottom:**
The lowest price is usually obvious only after the market has moved away from it.

**Incorrect Feedback — Otherwise profit is impossible:**
Investment results depend on many factors beyond choosing the lowest entry price.

**Incorrect Feedback — Prices never fall:**
Market prices can rise or fall, and regular investing does not prevent declines.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
Successful investing requires perfect market timing.

---

## Question 3 — Application

**Question:**
A planned monthly contribution occurs after the unit price falls. What happens immediately?

### Options

1. A profit becomes guaranteed
2. **The same contribution purchases more units**
3. The investment can no longer fall
4. All earlier purchases become losses

**Correct Answer:**
The same contribution purchases more units

**Correct-Answer Explanation:**
When the unit price is lower, a fixed contribution buys a greater number of units. The price may still continue falling.

**Incorrect Feedback — A profit becomes guaranteed:**
Buying at a lower price may help, but it does not guarantee the final result.

**Incorrect Feedback — All earlier purchases become losses:**
Earlier units remain owned, and their eventual result depends on later prices and any sale.

**Misconception Being Tested:**
An investor should automatically stop contributing whenever prices fall.
